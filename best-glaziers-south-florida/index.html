How to Evaluate the Best Commercial Glaziers in South Florida

Quick answer: Choosing the best commercial glazier in South Florida comes down to five verifiable criteria: 1) Active Florida CGC license, 2) bonding capacity matching your project size, 3) documented HVHZ/NOA submittal experience, 4) project portfolio in your specific building type, and 5) bid turnaround and response speed. Cheapest is almost never best - project delays from a low-bid glazier with permit problems can cost more than the entire glass package.

Criterion 1: License verification

Search the Florida DBPR online portal. Look for an active Certified General Contractor (CGC) or Certified Glass and Glazing Contractor license. Verify the license has no recent disciplinary actions. CGC #1531993 is ACG's - easy to verify.

Criterion 2: Bonding capacity

Ask for a bonding letter naming the bonding company and the per-project / aggregate limits. ACG provides performance and payment bonds on projects that require them, with capacity letters available during prequalification.

Criterion 3: NOA / FBC submittal experience

Florida glaziers with weak NOA experience cause permit delays - sometimes catastrophic ones. Ask for 3 recent HVHZ permit submittal references with the AHJ name and approval date. A glazier without this is a risk on HVHZ work.

Criterion 4: Project portfolio fit

A glazier who has installed 50 retail TIs is not the same as one who has installed 5 hospital curtain walls. Match the glazier's portfolio to your project type. ACG's project history spans restaurant, retail, hotel, medical, education, and high-end residential.

Criterion 5: Response speed

Bid turnaround tells you everything about a glazier's operations. Slow bid response (3+ weeks) usually means slow shop drawings, slow submittals, and slow installs. ACG returns bids in 48 hours on standard commercial plans.

Red flags to walk away from

1) No web presence or only a Facebook page. 2) Cash-only or 'discount for cash.' 3) Inability to provide a current license number. 4) No physical office address. 5) Recent BBB complaints around abandonment or non-completion. 6) Bid significantly below the next 3 bidders (this almost always means missing scope).

Why star ratings are the wrong signal for a glazing package

Most published "best glass company" lists mix two unrelated markets. One is retail glass service: shower enclosures, mirrors, home window replacement, auto glass. The other is commercial glazing subcontracting: engineered aluminum systems installed under a construction schedule and a permit review. A five-star retail reputation says nothing about whether a firm can produce sealed shop drawings or hold a curtain wall lead time.

Signals that do not predict commercial glazing performance:

  • Consumer review star averages and review volume
  • Years in business alone, absent projects of comparable system type
  • Directory badges and paid profile placements
  • A large showroom or retail storefront presence
  • Lowest unit price per square foot on a bid form

Signals that do predict it: license class, bonding, comparable project list by system and jurisdiction, shop drawing turnaround, approval documentation fluency, and closeout history.

The seven-point qualification checklist a GC should actually run

The five criteria above are the fast screen. The seven checks below are the full prequalification pass, and they are the ones that decide whether a glazing package holds its enclosure date.

1. License class and scope authority

  • Get the license number and verify it directly with the Florida Department of Business and Professional Regulation, not from a PDF the bidder sends you.
  • Confirm the class covers your scope. Florida law defines a specialty contractor as one whose scope is limited to a particular phase of construction established by board rule, per Florida Statutes 489.105.
  • Confirm the qualifying agent is an employee of the bidding entity.
  • ACG holds Florida license CGC 1531993 and performs installation in Florida only.

2. Bonding capacity, single and aggregate

  • Ask for both numbers: single project and aggregate.
  • Compare single-project capacity to your glazing scope value, not to the total project value.
  • Ask what is already committed against the aggregate this year.
  • ACG carries $3,000,000 single project and $6,000,000 aggregate bonding capacity.

3. Insurance limits and additional insured status

  • Match certificate limits against your subcontract exhibit before award, not at mobilization.
  • Check general liability, auto, workers compensation, and any required umbrella.
  • Confirm additional insured and waiver of subrogation endorsements are available.
  • ACG carries general liability at $1,000,000 per occurrence and $2,000,000 aggregate.

4. Product approval familiarity

  • Ask which approval path the bid assumes: statewide Florida Product Approval or Miami-Dade Notice of Acceptance.
  • Ask the bidder to name the approval documents for the systems they priced. [verify with product data sheet]
  • Confirm they know the testing protocols behind an NOA. Miami-Dade County states that in Protocol TAS 202, if the product is impact resistant then TAS 201 and TAS 203 will also be performed, and one specimen to TAS 202 is allowed, while non-impact products require three units, per Miami-Dade County product testing guidance.
  • Confirm awareness that fixed glazing used as an exterior component requires product approval in the High Velocity Hurricane Zone, per the HVHZ glass and glazing code text.

5. Shop drawing capability and turnaround

  • Ask who drafts: in-house, the supplier, or an outside consultant.
  • Ask for a current turnaround commitment in calendar days from award.
  • Ask to see a redacted sample drawing set from a comparable project.
  • Ask how they coordinate anchorage and adjacent-trade interfaces with the structural engineer.

6. Lead time management

  • Require written lead times by system type: storefront, curtain wall, impact windows, entrances, hardware. [verify with product data sheet]
  • Ask which components are the constraint on your specific configuration.
  • Ask what the recovery plan is if a fabrication date slips two weeks.
  • Re-confirm lead times at award, because bid-day numbers age quickly.

7. Closeout documentation

  • Manufacturer and installation warranties, with start dates defined.
  • As-built shop drawings reflecting field changes.
  • Approval or NOA records used for permitting.
  • Glass, finish, and hardware maintenance data.
  • Water test reports where the specification requires field testing.

A simple way to score bidders

Score each of the seven checks as pass, conditional, or fail, then apply two rules:

  1. Any fail on license, bonding, or insurance removes the bidder. Those are contract prerequisites, not preferences.
  2. Compare price only among remaining bidders. A low number from an unqualified firm is not a lower cost, it is deferred cost.

Where two bidders tie, break the tie on comparable project evidence in the same jurisdiction and the same system type.

Why Florida raises the bar on glazing qualification

Glazing selection carries more consequence in Florida than in most states, and the reason is documented in the historical record. NOAA counts 110 hurricane strikes on Florida between 1851 and 2004, including 35 major hurricanes of Category 3 or higher, per the National Hurricane Center state strike tabulation. Hurricane Andrew produced roughly $25 billion in damage and destroyed 25,524 homes while damaging 101,241 more, according to the NOAA National Hurricane Center report.

The failure mode that drove the code response was openings:

"Because many structures were destroyed when door or window failures allowed wind pressure to demolish roofs from the interior, codes need to emphasize appropriate door and window storm covers."
National Weather Service, Hurricane Andrew Natural Disaster Survey Report

That is why the code in force today, the Florida Building Code, 8th Edition (2023), effective December 31, 2023, treats glazing as a life-safety assembly. In the HVHZ counties of Miami-Dade and Broward, glazing within the lowest 30 feet of the exterior walls must resist large missile impact and glazing above that height must resist small missile impact, per the Florida Building Code HVHZ chapter. A glazing bidder who cannot discuss those requirements fluently is not ready for your permit review.

When a residential glass company is the right call, honestly

A commercial glazing subcontractor is the wrong hire for a lot of real work. If your need is on this list, call a local residential or retail glass shop instead. They will be faster and cheaper, and ACG does not perform any of it:

  • Shower enclosures and frameless shower doors
  • Mirrors, mirrored walls, and custom cut mirror
  • Single-family home window and door replacement
  • Insulated glass unit replacement in an existing residential window
  • Auto glass and windshield work
  • Tabletops, shelving, and decorative cut glass
  • Emergency board-up on a single storefront pane after hours
  • Screen enclosures and pool cage glass

Signals that you genuinely need a commercial Division 08 subcontractor instead:

  • The work is on a permitted commercial project with a design team and specifications
  • Scope includes storefront, curtain wall, window wall, or unitized systems
  • Shop drawings and product approval submittals are contract deliverables
  • Bonding, insurance limits, and a schedule with liquidated damages apply
  • The building sits in HVHZ or the Wind-Borne Debris Region and openings must be impact rated

Eight red flags in a glazing bid

  1. No license number offered without being asked twice.
  2. Bonding described as "available" with no capacity figure.
  3. A bid that omits shop drawings and engineering from the scope of work.
  4. No mention of which approval path was priced.
  5. Design pressures quoted from a brochure rather than the structural drawings.
  6. Lead times given as a single number for all systems.
  7. No comparable project in your jurisdiction and system type.
  8. A price far below the field with no explanation of what changed in the scope.

Frequently asked

How do I find a qualified commercial glazier in South Florida?

Start with the Florida DBPR license search - verify the contractor holds an active CGC or Certified Glass and Glazing license. Then check bonding capacity, HVHZ submittal experience, and portfolio in your project type.

What questions should I ask a commercial glazier before hiring?

Ask for: 1) license number and DBPR verification, 2) bonding letter, 3) three recent HVHZ project references with permit dates, 4) sample shop drawings from a comparable project, 5) workmanship warranty terms.

Why are the cheapest commercial glaziers risky?

Low bids usually mean missing scope (no NOA review fees, no shop drawings, no engineering, no hardware). Permit delays from a low-bid glazier with weak NOA experience can cost more than the entire glass package.

How fast should a commercial glazier respond to a bid request?

Qualified Florida commercial glaziers should return standard bids within 5-7 business days. Faster operations (like ACG) hit 48 hours on most plans. Slow response usually signals slow downstream operations.

Should I bid commercial glazing to 3 or 5 glaziers?

Three qualified bidders is the sweet spot for commercial glazing. Five invites unqualified low bidders. Pre-qualify by license, bonding, and portfolio before sending plans.

How do I choose the best commercial glazing contractor for a Florida project?

Score bidders on seven checks before price: Florida license class, single and aggregate bonding capacity, insurance limits against your subcontract, Florida Product Approval and Miami-Dade NOA familiarity, shop drawing capability and turnaround, written lead times by system type, and closeout documentation. Eliminate any bidder that fails license, bonding, or insurance, then compare price among the rest.

What license does a commercial glazing contractor need in Florida?

It depends on the scope. Florida distinguishes certified contractors from specialty contractors whose scope is limited to a particular phase of construction established by board rule. Verify the number and class directly with the Florida Department of Business and Professional Regulation, and confirm the qualifying agent works for the bidding entity. ACG holds Florida license CGC 1531993.

How much bonding capacity should a glazing subcontractor have?

Compare single-project bonding capacity to the value of the glazing scope, not the whole project, and ask how much of the aggregate is already committed. ACG carries $3,000,000 single project and $6,000,000 aggregate capacity, which covers most mid-market commercial glazing packages.

Is a highly rated local glass company good enough for commercial storefront work?

Usually not. Consumer star ratings measure retail service quality on shower doors, mirrors, and home window replacement. Commercial storefront and curtain wall work requires bonding, subcontract-level insurance, shop drawing production, product approval documentation, and schedule integration that retail glass shops are not structured to deliver.

When should I hire a residential glass company instead?

Hire a residential or retail glass shop for shower enclosures, mirrors, single-family window and door replacement, insulated glass unit replacement in existing residential windows, auto glass, tabletops, screen enclosures, and after-hours single-pane board-up. Those needs are served faster and cheaper by a retail shop, and ACG does not perform them.

What should I ask about lead times before awarding a glazing subcontract?

Ask for lead times in writing by system type, which component is the constraint on your configuration, what the recovery plan is if fabrication slips, and re-confirm the numbers at award rather than relying on bid-day figures. Actual durations vary by system, glass make-up, and hardware.

Does the same framework apply outside HVHZ counties?

Yes. The seven checks apply statewide. What changes is the approval path and impact requirement: Miami-Dade and Broward are HVHZ, while most other coastal Florida counties fall in the Wind-Borne Debris Region with a different documentation route to the same objective.
