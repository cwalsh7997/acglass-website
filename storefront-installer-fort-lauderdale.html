What commercial storefront means in Fort Lauderdale (HVHZ)

Fort Lauderdale is located in Broward County — one of Florida's two High Velocity Hurricane Zone (HVHZ) counties alongside Miami-Dade. This designation, established under the Florida Building Code following Hurricane Andrew and codified in FBC Section 1609.1.1, means that every exterior glazing system installed in a Fort Lauderdale commercial building must carry a current Miami-Dade Notice of Acceptance (NOA) issued by the Miami-Dade County Department of Regulatory and Economic Resources, Product Control Section.

An NOA for Fort Lauderdale commercial storefront confirms that the system has passed all three HVHZ testing protocols:

  • TAS 201 — Large-missile impact test: a 9-lb 2x4 wood projectile fired at 50 ft/sec. No penetration through the frame or glazing is permitted.
  • TAS 202 — Structural uniform static air pressure test confirming the system's rated design pressure (DP) in PSF, both positive (push) and negative (suction).
  • TAS 203 — Cyclic wind pressure loading: 9,000 pressure cycles simulating sustained hurricane-force gusting. The system must maintain air, water, and structural integrity through the full cycle count.

Unlike the rest of non-HVHZ Florida where the statewide Florida Product Approval (FPA) pathway governs, Broward County projects must use systems with Miami-Dade NOAs — a more rigorous approval pathway that establishes Fort Lauderdale as one of the technically demanding commercial glazing markets in the country.

Fort Lauderdale's commercial construction pipeline includes downtown Class A office and mixed-use tower development, Las Olas Boulevard hospitality and restaurant projects, Port Everglades-adjacent marine-commercial facilities, and an active suburban retail and multifamily sector across Broward County. Each of these project types involves HVHZ glazing packages with NOA documentation, DP analysis, and submittal packages that satisfy Broward County Building Division and City of Fort Lauderdale Building Services reviewers.

Code and permitting in Fort Lauderdale (HVHZ)

Commercial storefront in Fort Lauderdale is governed by the Florida Building Code (FBC), 8th Edition, with HVHZ provisions under FBC Section 1609. Permitting authority is the City of Fort Lauderdale Building Services Department for City projects, and Broward County Building Division for unincorporated county locations. HVHZ submittal requirements:

  • Current Miami-Dade NOA for every exterior glazing system — including the NOA number, issue and expiration dates, approved system configuration, maximum tested frame size, glass make-up, and anchorage requirements.
  • TAS 201 / 202 / 203 reference — NOAs cross-reference the three TAS test protocols. The submittal should note which NOA applies to each opening type (storefront vs. door vs. specialty), confirmed to be within the tested size envelope.
  • Design pressure analysis per ASCE 7 — Broward County building heights and coastal exposure (Category C or D) drive DP requirements that must be matched to the NOA's rated DP per the frame size and glass make-up specified.
  • Anchorage details per the NOA — substrate-specific fastener types, spacing, and edge distances. Broward's prevalent concrete and CMU substrates require NOA-compliant anchorage documentation in the submittal package.
  • Glass make-up specification — The NOA specifies the exact glass make-up (e.g., laminated with PVB interlayer, minimum thickness, configuration). Any deviation from the tested assembly voids the NOA coverage.
HVHZ — no exceptions. Broward County plan reviewers reject glazing submittals missing NOA documentation on first pass, regardless of project urgency or relationship. There is no variance pathway for HVHZ NOA requirements in Fort Lauderdale.
