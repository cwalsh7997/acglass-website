Folding (Bi-Fold) Glass Walls

Bi-fold folding glass wall systems for restaurants, hospitality, and indoor-outdoor commercial applications, including Florida Product Approvals.

PART 1 GENERAL

1.1 SUMMARY
A. Section includes bi-fold (folding) glass wall systems with 2-, 3-, 5-, 7-, and 8-panel configurations.

1.2 REFERENCES
A. AAMA 920 - Specification for Operating Cycle Performance for Side-Hinged Exterior Door Systems.
B. ASTM E283, E330, E331, E1886, E1996.
C. ANSI Z97.1.
D. Florida Building Code 8th Edition; Miami-Dade NOA for HVHZ projects.

1.3 PERFORMANCE REQUIREMENTS
A. Wind Load: Per ASCE 7-22 for project location.
B. Operating Cycles: Minimum 100,000 cycles without component failure per AAMA 920.
C. Impact Resistance (HVHZ): ASTM E1886 / E1996.

1.4 SUBMITTALS
A. Product Data, Shop Drawings (sealed by Florida PE), Florida Product Approval, Operating Hardware Cut Sheets, Sample of Hinges and Roller System.

1.5 QUALITY ASSURANCE
A. Manufacturer: Florida Product Approval listed for proposed system.
B. Installer: Manufacturer-authorized; minimum 5 installations of similar size in Florida.

1.6 DELIVERY, STORAGE, AND HANDLING
A. Deliver materials in manufacturer's original, unopened, undamaged containers with identification labels intact.
B. Store materials in a dry, ventilated location, off the ground, protected from weather, moisture, dust, and contact with dissimilar materials. Maintain factory-applied protective coverings until immediately prior to installation.
C. Handle glass per GANA Glazing Manual Section 13. Reject and replace any unit with cracked, chipped, or scratched glass. Do not store glass in direct sunlight; cover with non-staining, opaque material if outdoor storage is unavoidable.
D. Field-measure all openings prior to fabrication. Do not begin fabrication until field measurements are reconciled against shop drawings and recorded on the project Owner-Architect-Contractor (OAC) meeting log.

1.7 PROJECT CONDITIONS
A. Field Measurements: Verify dimensions of structural openings and tolerances at perimeter conditions before fabrication. Coordinate with the General Contractor for sequencing if openings will be modified after measurement.
B. Existing Conditions: Coordinate with adjacent trades - concrete, masonry, framing, sheathing, water-resistive barriers, flashings - to ensure proper sequence of work. Verify that perimeter substrate conditions are within manufacturer's installation tolerances before beginning fenestration work.
C. Coordination: Verify that openings, anchors, embeds, sealant joints, and water-resistive barrier terminations are installed and inspected before glazing system installation begins.

1.8 WARRANTY (PROJECT-SPECIFIC)
A. General Warranty: Warranties specified in this section do not deprive Owner of other rights Owner may have under other provisions of the Contract Documents.
B. Special Project Warranty: Submit a written warranty signed by the manufacturer and the installer covering failure of materials and workmanship for the period specified, beginning on the date of Substantial Completion. Warranty shall cover, without limitation, failure to meet performance requirements, structural failure under specified design loads, water and air infiltration in excess of specified rates, glass breakage from non-impact causes, and seal failure resulting in interior condensation between glass surfaces.

PART 2 PRODUCTS

2.1 MANUFACTURERS
A. Acceptable Manufacturers:
1. Euro-Wall Vistafold series (basis of design).
2. Other manufacturers with FPA / NOA documentation, approved prior to bid.

2.2 SYSTEM
A. Frame: Aluminum 6063-T6 with thermal break.
B. Glazing: 1-inch insulating glass with laminated impact glass for HVHZ.
C. Hardware: Stainless steel hinges, multipoint locking system, top-hung or bottom-rolling configuration as specified.
D. Sill: Low-profile or recessed track for ADA compliance where required.

PART 3 EXECUTION

3.1 INSTALLATION
A. Install per approved shop drawings and manufacturer instructions.
B. Verify structural header above the opening; folding wall is not load-bearing.
C. Adjust panels for smooth operation; lubricate per manufacturer.

3.2 FIELD QUALITY CONTROL
A. Demonstrate full operation through 5 cycles; no binding, dragging, or leakage.

3.3 WARRANTY
A. 5-year manufacturer's warranty on hardware; 2-year installer warranty.

3.4 EXAMINATION
A. Examine openings, substrates, structural support, anchorage, blocking, and conditions for compliance with manufacturer's requirements before beginning installation. Verify rough openings are plumb, square, and within manufacturer's tolerances.
B. Verify that field measurements match approved shop drawings.
C. Verify continuity of water-resistive barrier (WRB) and air barrier at the rough opening per the project's air-barrier and water-management details.
D. Proceed with installation only after unsatisfactory conditions have been corrected. Beginning installation indicates acceptance of substrate and opening conditions.

3.5 PREPARATION
A. Clean opening surfaces immediately before installation. Remove dust, debris, and loose particles.
B. Apply primers and sealant tapes per manufacturer's instructions where required for bond compatibility.
C. Coordinate with the General Contractor for any required flashings, pan flashings, sill pans, or membrane transitions at the rough opening before frame installation.

3.6 ADJUSTING
A. Adjust operating sashes, doors, hardware, and accessories for smooth, leak-proof operation. Re-lubricate per manufacturer recommendations.
B. Replace defective work that cannot be successfully repaired.

3.7 CLEANING AND PROTECTION
A. Remove labels, dirt, foreign substances, and excess sealant from finished surfaces using methods recommended by glass and frame manufacturers. Do not use abrasive cleaners, alkaline solutions, hydrofluoric-acid-based cleaners, or solvents that could damage glass coatings, anodized finishes, or PVDF coatings.
B. Replace glass that is broken, chipped, cracked, abraded, or damaged in any way before Substantial Completion.
C. Provide temporary protection to assemble units against staining, scratching, and damage during construction. Remove protection at Substantial Completion.

3.8 DEFICIENCY PROCEDURES
A. Documented Deficiencies: Record any deficiencies discovered during examination, installation, or commissioning in the project Owner-Architect-Contractor (OAC) log. Coordinate with General Contractor on corrective action and re-inspection.
B. Water Infiltration: Investigate any reported water infiltration following AAMA 511 forensic procedures. Document chain of custody for warranty claim purposes.

ACG Spec Writing Notes

Folding (bi-fold) glass walls are an operable, occupant-load-dependent system. The spec writer's most common error is treating a bi-fold like a fixed storefront - different structural load path, different sealant geometry, different hardware service life.

Florida-specific considerations

  • HVHZ (Miami-Dade, Broward): The system must carry both a Florida Product Approval AND a Miami-Dade NOA. The FPA alone is insufficient for permit submission inside the HVHZ.
  • Wind load: ASCE 7-22 wind loads on operable systems are calculated at the closed condition. Confirm shop drawings reflect closed-state loading; do not accept calculations based on open-state structural pathways.
  • Hurricane shutter coordination: Folding walls are typically the most extensive single opening in a building. Confirm whether a deployable shutter or panel system is required by the AHJ for the occupancy class - many restaurant occupancies are exempt; multifamily is usually not.
  • Threshold and sill geometry: Florida FBC accessibility requirements (FBC-A 404.2.5) limit threshold height to 1/2 inch maximum unless an exception applies. Recessed sill detailing requires coordination with the slab depression and waterproofing layer.

Common pitfalls we see

  • Header structural verification: The folding wall is NOT load-bearing. Architect must specify a structural header sized for snow, dead, and wind loads. Most failures we have seen are from undersized or under-deflected headers that bind the operation after settlement.
  • Hardware service-life mismatch: AAMA 920 calls out 100,000 cycles for hinged systems. Restaurant occupancies routinely exceed 50,000 cycles in the first three years. Specify a manufacturer with documented commercial-cycle test data, not residential cycle data.
  • Threshold drainage: The recessed-sill detail must include drainage to daylight or to a sub-floor weep system. Sealing a recessed track without drainage creates a water trap.
  • Panel weight on lateral framing: Multi-panel folds (5-, 7-, 8-panel) impose significant point loads on the king post or jamb. Coordinate with the structural engineer of record before final hardware selection.

Submittal review checklist

  • Confirm panel count, swing direction, stacking side, and threshold type match the shop drawing legend on every elevation.
  • Verify the Florida PE seal on shop drawings is current and the engineer is licensed in the project AHJ.
  • Confirm hardware sample (hinge plus roller plus latch) is provided, not just a cut sheet.
  • Confirm operating-cycle test data is in the submittal package, dated within the last 5 years.
  • For HVHZ: verify both FPA and NOA documents are current and the configuration on the project matches the tested configuration.
