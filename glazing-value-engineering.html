Glazing Value Engineering: Approved-Equal Substitution for FL GCs

Done correctly, VE saves real money. Done incorrectly, VE creates warranty disputes and field rework that costs more than the savings. The six categories below are where Florida commercial glazing scope actually has room to optimize without breaking code, NOA, or warranty. Category 6 is module-size / layout VE: same performance class, fewer pieces.

By Connor Walsh - FL CGC #1531993 - (772) 486-7711 - Updated 2026-09-12

Six VE Categories That Actually Work

These are the substitution categories ACG runs through formal VE analysis on Florida commercial projects. Each one preserves design pressure, NOA compliance, thermal performance, and aesthetic intent, and produces documented architect signoff.

1. IGU Make-Up Substitution

Specs often default to laminated lites on both sides of the IGU. The VE submittal documents which openings qualify and which retain laminated.

2. Frame Depth Optimization

2.5-inch and 4-inch storefront systems both meet most Florida commercial design pressures. VE submittal includes structural deflection analysis and NOA cross-reference.

3. Approved-Equal Manufacturer Substitution

When the spec names a system from outside ACG's seven-manufacturer install list, ACG submits an approved-equal package from ESWindows, Euro-Wall, PGT, Allegion, TGP, Slimpact, or Aldora. Documentation includes side-by-side performance comparison, NOA equivalence, finish match samples, and architect approval form.

4. Hardware Consolidation

Specs often list four or five different closer models, three latch types, and inconsistent keying. Allegion installation experience streamlines the substitution review.

5. Sealant Specification

VE submittal includes sealant manufacturer compatibility letter for the substituted application.

6. Module Size / Layout Optimization (not a glass downgrade)

This is the B16 addition. Module-size VE changes how the same elevation is divided into lites and mullions. It does not mean thinner glass, lower design pressure, weaker interlayer, or a less expensive NOA path. The worked comparison below is a template for scope-matched layouts.

What moves: mullion count, IGU unit count, perimeter sealant length, field joint count, and often fabrication / install hours.
What must stay equal: required design pressure class, Florida Product Approval / NOA path for the assembly, thermal targets (U / SHGC as specified), safety glazing locations, and architect aesthetic intent (sightline language, finish, and overall module rhythm).

How to run the comparison without inventing savings

  1. Freeze the elevation outline and the performance class (DP, thermal, safety glazing marks).
  2. Draw Option A (as-specified module rhythm) and Option B (wider modules within the approved system limits).
  3. Count mullions, IGU units, and field joints for both options on the same opening list.
  4. Price both options from the same supplier basis date. Record the delta in the VE form. If you do not have that budget basis yet, leave the dollar / percent cells blank.
  5. Attach the NOA / FL PA excerpt that confirms Option B lite sizes and anchors still fall inside the approved assembly.

Keep savings language qualitative only until two approved conceptual layouts and a dated supplier budget basis exist for the same elevation.

What Doesn't Get Value-Engineered

Equally important: where ACG does not propose VE. These categories are where the apparent savings create field problems that cost more than the savings.

  • NOA / Florida Product Approval. Substituting a product with a less-rigorous approval to save money is the fastest way to fail final inspection.
  • Anchor type and spacing. NOAs prescribe exact anchor diameter, spacing, and embedment. Off-spec anchors are a primary cause of envelope warranty disputes.
  • Sealant on a wet substrate. Polyurethane is incompatible with green concrete, lime-cured stucco, and unwashed substrates. Saving on sealant by skipping compatibility verification is a multi-year leak risk.
  • Tempering downgrade in safety locations. ANSI Z97.1 and CPSC 16 CFR 1201 mandate safety glazing at doors, sidelights, low panels, and stair guards. No VE.
  • Structural silicone with off-spec gaskets. Sealant warranties require the manufacturer's approved gasket and primer system. Substituting a "compatible" gasket from a different vendor voids warranty.
  • Glass performance downgrade dressed up as "module VE." Changing module size is not permission to drop laminated to monolithic, drop Low-E class, or leave the approved assembly. If the glass class changes, it is a different VE item with its own performance table.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is glazing value engineering?

The formal process of proposing alternate systems, glass make-ups, hardware, sealants, or module layouts that maintain code compliance, design pressure, thermal performance, and aesthetic intent while reducing cost or schedule.

What's the difference between VE and substitution?

Substitution is replacement. VE is documented analysis showing the substitution maintains performance with quantified cost or schedule benefit and architect approval.

Can a GC require value engineering on a glazing scope?

Yes. Most large Florida commercial projects include a VE phase between award and submittal.

What gets value-engineered most often?

IGU make-up, frame depth, approved-equal manufacturer substitution, hardware consolidation, sealant grade, and module-size / layout optimization, in that order on many Florida storefront packages.

Is changing module size the same as downgrading glass?

No. Module-size VE keeps the performance class and approval path and changes how the elevation is divided. A glass downgrade is a separate VE item and must show equal or better performance in the comparison table.

Does value engineering void the manufacturer warranty?

Not when done correctly. Substituted product must be on the manufacturer's installer list, NOA must cover the substitution, sealant must be compatible, and the architect must approve in writing.

Have a Florida commercial project where the glazing scope needs a VE pass, including a module-layout alternative? Send the elevations and the door and window schedule. Mark material-only vs furnish-and-install.
