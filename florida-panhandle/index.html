Why the Florida Panhandle is its own commercial glazing market.

The Panhandle is geographically and architecturally distinct from peninsular Florida. Wind loads run 130 to 150 mph on the coast. The Wind-Borne Debris Region covers every coastal county. Hurricane Ivan (2004), Hurricane Michael (2018, the strongest US landfall since Andrew), and Hurricane Sally (2020) rewrote what coastal commercial construction practices the region can sustain — and Bay County, Gulf County, and Escambia County inspectors enforce post-storm code requirements at a level of scrutiny most peninsular Florida markets don't see.

The 30A corridor in Walton County is its own architectural universe — New Urbanist beach towns (Seaside, Rosemary Beach, Alys Beach, WaterColor, WaterSound) where every commercial storefront clears an Architectural Review Board before it gets permitted. Rosemary Beach's town architect, Alys Beach's Khoury Vogt Architects, Seaside's ARB — each one cares as much about a window's sightline width and finish color as the impact NOA.

Tallahassee is a different market entirely — state capitol, FSU and FAMU, healthcare, interior wind loads, no WBDR. The submittal rigor on state and university work is what differentiates winners from losers, not the lowest bid.

ACG is a Florida-statewide licensed glazing contractor (CGC #1531993) with the impact NOA library, anchor calc engineering, architectural review submittal experience, and Florida Product Approval coverage the Panhandle requires across all eight commercial counties.
