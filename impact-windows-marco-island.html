The Market

Marco Island is a commercial glazing market.

Marco Island is Florida's second-largest barrier island, covering 17.5 square miles off Collier County's southwest coast. The commercial market is hospitality-heavy: JW Marriott Marco Island, Marriott Marco Island, The Hilton, multiple boutique beach resorts, waterfront restaurants, and an extensive Class A and luxury condominium footprint. Retail centers include Marco Town Center and the Shops at Old Marco.

Marco Island is entirely in Exposure Category D - the highest wind-load designation outside HVHZ. Hurricane Ian (September 2022) made landfall at Cayo Costa just 40 miles north and brought catastrophic damage throughout Southwest Florida including Marco Island. Hurricane Milton (October 2024) added further commercial impact. The rebuild and hardening cycle has emphasized full FBC 8th Edition compliance, Exposure D documentation, and salt-air-tolerant finishes (anodized aluminum or PVDF powder-coat effectively standard).

ACG serves Marco Island from our Naples office - approximately 20 miles north. Our ESWindows flagship partnership provides Exposure D-rated commercial storefront, window wall, and SGD-2020 impact sliding door systems with the architectural-grade finishes Marco Island resort and luxury condo projects require. For the oceanfront hospitality and amenity spaces that define Marco Island's commercial market, we install Euro-Wall folding and multi-slide systems - the same premium moving-wall product on Atlantic Fields.

Marco Island FAQ

Common questions - answered.

Does Marco Island require impact windows on commercial and multifamily buildings?

Yes. Marco Island is in Collier County's wind-borne debris region and is entirely Exposure Category D. Florida Building Code 8th Edition requires impact-rated glazing or approved shutter systems on every commercial opening. Post-Hurricane Ian and Milton, enforcement has been especially rigorous on rebuild permits.

What design pressures apply to Marco Island commercial projects?

Marco Island is entirely Exposure Category D. DP60 to DP80+ is typical depending on building height and Gulf proximity. Oceanfront high-rise condos and resort towers often require DP80+ with laminated SGP interlayers and structural silicone glazing at upper floors. Florida Product Approval documentation must explicitly confirm Exposure D suitability.

How did Hurricanes Ian and Milton affect Marco Island commercial buildings?

Hurricane Ian (September 2022) made landfall at Cayo Costa 40 miles north of Marco Island and brought catastrophic wind and storm surge to the island. Hurricane Milton (October 2024) added further commercial impact. The rebuild and hardening cycle since has emphasized full FBC Exposure D compliance, NOA-level product specifications, and salt-air-tolerant finish selections across resort, condo, and retail commercial properties.
