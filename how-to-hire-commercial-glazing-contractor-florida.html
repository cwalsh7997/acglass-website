How to Hire a Commercial Glazing Contractor in Florida

Bottom line. Vet on license, bonding, NOA / Florida Product Approval, insurance, manufacturer certifications, references, safety record, submittal capability, and field supervision. Price is the last filter, not the first. This is the 9-point checklist Florida GCs use on commercial work.

Written by American Commercial Glass - FL CGC #1531993 - Published 2026-05-15

Why the Checklist Exists

On commercial projects in Florida, glazing scope is one of the highest-risk packages a GC awards. The envelope sees hurricane loads, code review, owner-direct punchlist scrutiny, and water-intrusion liability that extends years past closeout. A glazing contractor that fails on submittals, NOA, or field execution costs more in rework and schedule slip than the savings on the original bid.

The 9-point checklist below mirrors the prequalification process used by Florida's mid- and large-tier general contractors. Use it on every storefront, impact window, curtainwall, multi-slide door, and fire-rated glazing scope.

The 9-Point Checklist

1. Verify Florida CGC or specialty license

Confirm an active Florida Certified General Contractor (CGC) license, or the appropriate specialty license, on the Florida DBPR portal. License lapses, suspensions, and prior disciplinary action are public. ACG operates under CGC #1531993.

2. Confirm bonding capacity

Request a bond letter on the surety's letterhead, dated within the past 90 days, listing single-project and aggregate limits. Treasury-listed sureties only. Confirm the sub is bondable on commercial scopes with headroom for change orders and retainage exposure. ACG provides performance and payment bonds on projects that require them.

3. Validate NOA and Florida Product Approval

For HVHZ jurisdictions (Miami-Dade and Broward), every glazing product on the project must carry a current Miami-Dade NOA. Statewide HVHZ-adjacent work uses Florida Product Approval under the Florida Building Code (FBC). Ask the bidder to attach NOA / FL Approval numbers to each line item on their bid. If they cannot, that is the answer.

4. Check insurance limits and additional insured posture

Request the COI before award. Verify the producer (broker) directly if the project is over $2M.

5. Confirm manufacturer certifications

Direct manufacturer certification matters because most manufacturer warranties (and many NOAs) require certified installation. For commercial scope in Florida, the certifications worth verifying are:

  • ESWindows - impact windows, curtainwall, structural systems
  • Euro-Wall - multi-slide, bifold, pivot, lift-and-slide
  • PGT - impact windows and doors
  • Allegion - commercial door hardware
  • TGP - fire-rated glass and framing
  • Slimpact - slim-profile impact systems

6. Request three project references on similar scope

References should be from GCs (not owners), within the past 24 months, on dollar-size and product-type matched to your scope. Speak to the project manager and the superintendent - not the salesperson who introduced the bidder. Ask: did the sub hit submittal dates, did they ship on the original schedule, what was the change-order ratio, and did closeout punch resolve inside 30 days.

7. Audit safety record

Request the Experience Modification Rate (EMR), the most recent OSHA 300 logs, and any DART history. EMR over 1.0 is a flag on most commercial bids; over 1.25 is disqualifying on most owner-direct work. ACG provides EMR and OSHA 300 documentation during prequalification.

8. Review submittal capability

Ask for a sample shop-drawing package with NOA cross-references and a sample submittal log. Submittal quality is a leading indicator of field execution quality - sloppy submittals predict sloppy work. Look for: NOA referenced on every detail, structural calcs sealed by a Florida-registered engineer, and clear interface details with adjacent trades.

9. Confirm field supervision structure

Identify the named project manager and named superintendent before contract execution. Confirm in writing that they will be assigned to your project. Ask how many concurrent projects each is currently supervising - three is the practical maximum for a Florida glazing sub before quality degrades. Ask how the contractor handles supervisor turnover mid-project.

Red Flags

  • Bid arrives without NOA numbers attached to each product line.
  • Bidder cannot produce a current bond letter within 48 hours of request.
  • Insurance COI is older than 30 days or limits are below the threshold above.
  • References declined or only owner-side references provided (no GC PMs).
  • Shop-drawing samples are residential-grade or vendor-supplied without contractor markup.
  • EMR not disclosed.
  • Single PM listed across five or more concurrent projects.
  • Manufacturer certifications cannot be verified directly with the manufacturer.
  • License has lapses, suspensions, or settled DBPR complaints in the past three years.

Frequently Asked Questions

What license does a commercial glazing contractor need in Florida?

A Certified General Contractor (CGC) license through the Florida DBPR is the most common credential. A specialty Glass and Glazing (CGI) license also qualifies. ACG operates under CGC #1531993.

What insurance limits should a commercial glazing subcontractor carry?

Florida commercial standard: $1M/$2M general liability, $5M umbrella, statutory workers comp, $1M auto. Projects over $5M typically require $10M umbrella.

What is a Miami-Dade NOA and when is it required?

A Notice of Acceptance is a Miami-Dade-issued product approval certifying TAS 201 / 202 / 203 compliance. Required in Miami-Dade and Broward Counties; commonly specified statewide.

How long does the glazing contractor selection process typically take?

14-30 days for private commercial scope under $2M; 30-60 days for public-bid and federally funded work.

Should I hire the lowest bidder?

On glazing scope, no - compare scope inclusions line-by-line before comparing price. Excluded items become extras, and rework from underbid scope is the most extensive hidden cost on the envelope package.

How many manufacturer certifications should a contractor hold?

At a minimum, direct certification with the manufacturers whose products are specified on your project. A glazing sub with six or more current manufacturer certifications is investing in their craft.
