Code Compliance

Tampa Bay Building Code & Hurricane Zone Requirements

Hillsborough County is not designated as a High Velocity Hurricane Zone (HVHZ), but don't let that fool you. Tampa Bay's coastal and near-coastal areas face significant wind design pressures. The Florida Building Code (FBC 7th Edition, 2023) requires commercial glazing in high-wind areas to resist design wind speeds of 150+ mph in some locations. This means impact-rated or wind-borne debris-protected glazing is frequently required, especially for ground-floor retail, tall buildings, and facilities within 1 mile of the coast.

ACG has installed hundreds of impact-rated systems in Miami-Dade County's HVHZ (the strictest code jurisdiction in North America) and brings that same expertise to every Tampa Bay project. We know the nuances: how to achieve wind pressure ratings, which products meet Miami-Dade NOA requirements, and how to coordinate with Hillsborough County's plan reviewers and inspectors. For Tampa Bay GCs and developers, that South Florida pedigree means faster permit approval, fewer RFIs, and installations that sail through final inspections.

Our team manages plan review timelines, submits all required documentation (wind pressure test data, product certifications, installation details), and schedules inspections with the county. Whether your project requires impact-rated glass, wind-borne debris protection, or standard FBC compliance, ACG delivers the code documentation and field-ready installation that Tampa Bay code officials expect.

FAQ

Frequently Asked Questions

Does ACG serve St. Petersburg and Clearwater?

Yes. ACG provides commercial glazing services throughout the Tampa Bay metropolitan area, including Tampa, St. Petersburg, Clearwater, Largo, Brandon, and Sarasota.

Does Tampa require impact-rated glazing?

Impact requirements in Tampa Bay are determined by the project's wind zone per the Florida Building Code. Coastal areas and high-rises typically require impact-rated or protected glazing. ACG installs code-compliant systems for all Tampa Bay wind zones.

What commercial glazing systems does ACG install in Tampa?

ACG installs a complete range of commercial glazing systems in Tampa Bay: commercial storefront (ES-8000/ES-9000 aluminum framing), impact-rated windows and doors (Miami-Dade NOA products for FBC compliance), window wall systems (floor-to-ceiling vision glass for multifamily and high-rise), sliding and folding glass (Eurowall MultiSlide and VistaFold for hospitality), fire-rated glazing (TGP/SAFTI systems), and automatic entrance operators (Allegion/Stanley ADA-compliant systems).

How does ACG handle hurricane code compliance for Tampa Bay projects?

ACG manages full code compliance from design through inspection. We review project specifications against the Florida Building Code (FBC 7th Edition), determine wind zone requirements and design pressures for your location, source Miami-Dade County NOA-approved products when impact protection is required, provide wind pressure test data and product certifications for plan review, coordinate with Hillsborough County building officials, and deliver installations that pass final inspection. Our South Florida HVHZ experience means we know exactly what inspectors expect and how to document compliance.

What is ACG's typical timeline for commercial glazing projects in Tampa?

Timeline varies by scope and project size. Retail storefront installations typically take 2-4 weeks from material arrival to completion. Window wall projects on mid-rise or high-rise buildings typically require 4-8 weeks depending on floor count and coordination with other trades. We work closely with GC schedules, coordinate material delivery with construction phasing, and provide weekly progress updates. We'll give you a project-specific schedule during the bid phase.

Does ACG provide value engineering for Tampa glazing projects?

Yes. ACG reviews glazing specifications during the bid phase and can often identify cost savings without compromising performance. This might include optimizing frame profiles, sourcing equivalent-performing products at lower cost, or finding efficiency gains in installation sequencing. We'll present options during bid review and let the team decide. Value engineering is especially helpful on large projects where small optimizations across multiple floors add up.
