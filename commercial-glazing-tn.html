Why laminated glass for Tennessee commercial work

A laminated glass assembly is two glass lites bonded by a PVB (polyvinyl butyral) or SentryGlas interlayer. The interlayer is what creates the performance gap between laminated and monolithic glass:

  • Acoustic — STC 35-45+ depending on lite thickness and interlayer thickness. A monolithic 1/4" lite tops out around STC 30. For Class-A office, hotel, healthcare, and high-end multifamily, the acoustic difference is the difference between a usable interior and one that requires sound-masking retrofit.
  • UV blockage — 99% of UV is blocked at the interlayer. Monolithic glass blocks roughly 25%. Interior finishes, artwork, fabrics, and merchandising last materially longer.
  • Safety — the interlayer holds glass together upon breakage. Meets ANSI Z97.1 and CPSC 16 CFR 1201 Category II safety glazing for hazardous locations.
  • Security — defeats smash-and-grab forced entry. Bank branches, retail, education, and healthcare specify laminated as baseline security glazing.
  • Thermal — paired with a low-E coating and an argon-filled IGU cavity, laminated glass delivers high-performance thermal control while keeping the four other categories above intact.

A standard IGU solves thermal, and nothing else. A monolithic lite solves nothing well. A laminated assembly with the right interlayer thickness and the right IGU pairing solves all five simultaneously, which is why it is the dominant Class-A specification across Tennessee commercial markets.

Code and standards in Tennessee

Tennessee commercial glazing must comply with:

  • 2018 International Building Code (IBC) as adopted at the state level with local jurisdictional amendments — structural-glazing, safety-glazing, and fire-rated assembly requirements.
  • ANSI Z97.1 — safety glazing impact and breakage standards.
  • CPSC 16 CFR 1201 — federal safety glazing in hazardous locations.
  • 2018 IECC commercial provisions — thermal envelope and energy code.
  • UL 9, UL 10B, UL 10C, UL 263 — fire-rated glazing where life-safety code requires.
  • ASHRAE 90.1 — commercial building energy standard.

ACG specifies laminated assemblies engineered for the project’s wind load (per ASCE 7), thermal envelope (per IECC and ASHRAE 90.1), safety glazing locations (per IBC Chapter 24 / Z97.1), and acoustic/security requirements where the owner has additional standards.

What is laminated glass and why is it used on Tennessee commercial buildings?

The interlayer holds glass together upon impact, blocks 99% of UV, reaches STC 35-45 depending on thickness, and resists forced entry.
