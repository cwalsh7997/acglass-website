Technical Standards

What HVHZ Actually Means
for Your Glazing Scope.

High Velocity Hurricane Zone (HVHZ) is a Florida Building Code designation that applies to all of Miami-Dade and Broward counties. It was established after Hurricane Andrew (1992) exposed catastrophic failures in standard window and door systems. HVHZ requirements have remained among the most stringent building envelope standards in the world.

The critical distinction GCs must understand: HVHZ requires Miami-Dade NOA (Notice of Acceptance), not a standard Florida Product Approval (FL#). These are two separate approvals issued by two separate authorities. A glazing product can hold a valid FL approval number and still be prohibited from use in Miami-Dade County because it lacks the corresponding NOA.

Miami-Dade NOA testing follows TAS (Testing Application Standard) protocols — TAS 201 (impact), TAS 202 (uniform static air pressure), and TAS 203 (cyclic wind pressure loading). TAS testing is more demanding than the ASTM E1886/E1996 protocols used for standard FL approvals. Large missile impact resistance (9-lb 2x4 at 50 fps), cyclic pressure sequencing, and water infiltration testing at elevated pressures are all required before Miami-Dade BCCO issues an NOA.

Common Questions

Miami Glazing FAQ.

Does ACG work in HVHZ areas like Miami-Dade? +

Yes. ACG is fully certified to work in Florida's High Velocity Hurricane Zone (HVHZ), which covers all of Miami-Dade and Broward counties. Every system we install in HVHZ carries the required Miami-Dade Notice of Acceptance (NOA) — not just a general Florida Product Approval. Our submittal packages include TAS-tested documentation, product approval numbers, and engineer-of-record sign-off built to pass Miami-Dade plan review on the first submission.

What glazing systems have Miami-Dade NOA approval? +

ACG installs multiple systems carrying Miami-Dade NOA approval, including ESWindows commercial series (NOA tested to TAS 201/202/203), CGI Windows commercial series, and Slimpact impact systems. For storefront and curtainwall, we source Eurowall and YKK AP products with current Miami-Dade NOA numbers on file. We do not substitute NOA-approved products with standard Florida Product Approvals when working in Miami-Dade County.

What is the difference between a Florida Product Approval and a Miami-Dade NOA? +

Florida Product Approval (FL#) is the statewide approval issued by the Florida Department of Business and Professional Regulation — sufficient for most of Florida. Miami-Dade NOA (Notice of Acceptance) is a separate, more rigorous county-level approval required specifically for HVHZ work in Miami-Dade and Broward counties. NOA testing follows TAS protocols — TAS 201 (large missile impact), TAS 202 (static pressure), TAS 203 (cyclic pressure) — which are more demanding than the ASTM tests used for FL approvals. A glazing sub showing up in Miami-Dade with only a FL approval number cannot legally install that system in HVHZ. This is one of the most common compliance failures ACG is called in to resolve.
