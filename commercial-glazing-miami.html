Code Compliance

Miami-Dade HVHZ Building Code Requirements

Miami-Dade County is the epicenter of the High Velocity Hurricane Zone (HVHZ) — the most stringent building code jurisdiction in the United States. Every commercial glazing system must carry a Miami-Dade Notice of Acceptance (NOA) and pass large missile impact testing at design wind speeds up to 195 mph.

ACG was built for the HVHZ. Our team has decades of combined experience navigating Miami-Dade’s permit review process, product approval requirements, and inspection protocols. Every system we install meets or exceeds HVHZ standards with full engineering documentation. The Florida Building Code 7th Edition (2023) plus Miami-Dade’s local amendments govern all commercial construction.

FAQ

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the HVHZ and how does it affect glazing in Miami?

The High Velocity Hurricane Zone covers all of Miami-Dade and Broward counties. It requires impact-rated glazing or approved shutter systems on all commercial buildings. Products must carry a Miami-Dade Notice of Acceptance, and installations face enhanced inspection requirements.

Does ACG install curtainwall on Miami high-rise projects?

Yes. ACG provides curtainwall installation for high-rise projects throughout Miami-Dade, including unitized and stick-built systems. We coordinate with structural engineers and building envelope consultants to meet South Florida's wind load and impact requirements.

How fast can ACG respond to a bid request in Miami?

ACG typically provides preliminary budgets within 48-72 hours and detailed bids within 5-7 business days for standard commercial projects in Miami-Dade County.

What glazing systems does ACG install in Miami?

ACG installs HVHZ-compliant storefront (ES-8000/ES-9000), curtainwall, window wall, sliding and folding glass walls (Eurowall MultiSlide, VistaFold), fire-rated glazing (TGP/SAFTI), and automatic entrances (Allegion/Stanley). Every system carries Miami-Dade NOA approval with large missile impact certification.

What is the typical permit timeline for Miami-Dade glazing projects?

Miami-Dade HVHZ permit review typically runs 3–6 weeks for commercial glazing. ACG submits complete NOA documentation and engineering packages upfront to minimize revision cycles. Storefront installation runs 2–4 weeks; curtainwall and window wall range from 6–14 weeks depending on project scope.

Does ACG provide value engineering for Miami projects?

Yes. ACG provides value engineering during preconstruction to help GCs and developers optimize glazing budgets while maintaining full HVHZ compliance. This includes alternative NOA-approved system selections, glass makeup optimization, and installation sequencing that reduces costs without compromising Miami-Dade code requirements.
