FAQ

Questions GCs ask before they bid.

What is HVHZ and where does it apply?+

HVHZ (High-Velocity Hurricane Zone) applies in Miami-Dade and Broward counties under the Florida Building Code. HVHZ requires impact-rated systems with active Miami-Dade NOA approvals. ACG is experienced in HVHZ submittal and installation protocols and carries manufacturer NOAs on every HVHZ project.

What is a DP rating?+

DP (Design Pressure) rating specifies the structural and water-resistance capacity of a window or door system at a given building elevation. Higher floors and coastal exposures require higher DP ratings. ACG engineers DP-appropriate system selection from shop-drawing phase forward.

What is the difference between impact and hurricane-rated?+

"Impact" means the system has passed large-missile impact testing (ASTM E1886/E1996). "Hurricane-rated" is a more general marketing term. For code compliance in Florida - especially HVHZ - the spec should always say "impact-rated" and reference the NOA or Florida Product Approval.

Can ACG supply impact glazing for a GC in a non-HVHZ county?+

Yes. Florida Product Approval applies statewide. We install impact glazing in Lee, Collier, Palm Beach, Martin, St. Lucie, Hillsborough, Pinellas, and every county between.

How long are typical lead times on commercial impact systems?+

Standard impact storefront runs 6-10 weeks from approved shop drawings. Large-missile tested window wall 8-12 weeks. Custom impact sliders 10-14 weeks. ACG confirms firm lead times with a bid submission.

Related questions

What is HVHZ glazing?

HVHZ stands for High-Velocity Hurricane Zone, covering Miami-Dade and Broward counties only. Glazing in these zones must pass large-missile impact and cyclic pressure testing and hold HVHZ Florida Product Approval or a Miami-Dade NOA. American Commercial Glass has Florida HVHZ Miami-Dade NOA compliance experience on commercial projects.

Are impact windows required in Florida?

In wind-borne debris regions - most of Florida's coastline, including all HVHZ counties - building code requires impact-rated glazing or approved opening protection. American Commercial Glass installs impact-rated commercial storefront, window, and door systems that meet Florida Building Code Section 1609 and HVHZ requirements through partners like ESWindows, PGT, and Slimpact.

Do commercial buildings need impact glazing in Florida?

Commercial buildings in wind-borne debris regions and HVHZ counties must protect all glazed openings with impact-rated systems or approved opening protection under Florida Building Code Section 1609. American Commercial Glass installs impact-rated commercial storefront, curtainwall, and door systems and has Florida HVHZ and Miami-Dade NOA compliance experience.
