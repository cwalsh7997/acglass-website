Broward HVHZ Context

Broward HVHZ. NOA is the deliverable, not the opening.

Broward County is in Florida's High-Velocity Hurricane Zone. The HVHZ designation is not a marketing term — it is a code chapter in the Florida Building Code that imposes specific Miami-Dade NOA documentation, installation, and inspection requirements on every commercial opening. A folding wall, multi-slide door, or pivot door installed in Fort Lauderdale must carry a current Miami-Dade NOA covering the specific configuration and glazing make-up of the opening, and the installation must follow the NOA installation instructions exactly. The Broward Building Code Services department verifies this at permit and at inspection.

The most common reason a Euro-Wall installation in Broward is delayed at inspection is missing or mis-matched NOA documentation. The NOA at submittal time does not match the configuration on the shop drawings, or the installation in the field does not match the NOA installation instructions, and the inspector flags the assembly. ACG's submittal discipline is built around this risk. The Euro-Wall NOAs for SGD2020, Vistafold, and the pivot products are pulled current at submittal, the shop drawings are aligned with the NOA configuration the owner approved, and the field installation is documented against the NOA install instructions with a photo log the GC can attach to the inspection request.

For Las Olas Marina, oceanfront, and direct water exposure sites, the design pressure requirement runs higher than inland Broward. Exposure D wind pressures apply on direct ocean exposure. Euro-Wall has Vistafold and SGD2020 configurations rated for these exposures, but the configuration on the shop drawings must match the exposure called out in the structural drawings. ACG's pre-construction process catches this alignment in the bid review — before the project is awarded, not after fabrication is in motion.

The restaurant operator timeline is the other constraint that defines Broward Euro-Wall projects. Most Fort Lauderdale restaurant openings carry a lease commencement and a build-out deadline that is not flexible. The Euro-Wall opening is on the critical path. ACG's lead time for an HVHZ Vistafold is 8-12 weeks from approved shop drawings, and the install runs 2-4 days for a typical 30-40 LF opening. The 48-hour bid turnaround that ACG runs on Broward restaurant work is designed to fit the operator's timeline at the front of the project, not at the end.
