Scope Boundary

What Division 08 Actually Covers, and Where the Boundary Sits.

Division 08 in CSI MasterFormat is Openings. The glazier's scope is defined by the specification sections inside that division, not by the phrase "glass work." Scope confusion is a leading source of glazing change orders, so write the boundary into the subcontract.

Typically inside the commercial glazier's scope

  • Aluminum storefront systems and entrance framing
  • Curtain wall and window wall assemblies, stick-built or unitized
  • Impact-rated glazing and hurricane-rated fixed and operable openings
  • Multi-slide and folding door systems
  • Fire-rated glass and fire-rated framed assemblies
  • Aluminum entrances, all-glass entrances, and associated door hardware
  • Glazing gaskets, structural silicone, perimeter sealant at the glazing interface
  • Shop drawings, product approval documentation, and glazing closeout submittals

Typically outside it, and worth confirming in writing

  • Structural backup steel, embeds, and blocking
  • Waterproofing transitions and air barrier tie-ins beyond the frame perimeter
  • Interior glass partitions when specified in Division 10
  • Final cleaning of glass after other trades finish overhead work
HVHZ Code

What Changes in Miami-Dade and Broward Counties.

The High Velocity Hurricane Zone is the most demanding wind and impact regime in the Florida Building Code, and it applies to Miami-Dade and Broward counties. The current code edition in force is the Florida Building Code, 8th Edition (2023), effective December 31, 2023.

Two HVHZ provisions drive almost every glazing decision on a South Florida exterior:

  • Missile impact by height. Per the HVHZ chapter on exterior walls, cladding, surfacing, and glazing within the lowest 30 feet of the exterior building walls must resist large missile impacts, and glazing above the lowest 30 feet must resist small missile impacts, as published by the Florida Building Commission HVHZ code text.
  • Product approval is mandatory, not optional. Fixed glazing used as an exterior component requires product approval, and glass block requires product approval, per the HVHZ windows, doors, glass and glazing code text.

Outside Miami-Dade and Broward, most coastal Florida jurisdictions fall in the Wind-Borne Debris Region. The impact protection concept is similar but the documentation path differs. Design pressures are set by the architect or engineer of record and shown on the drawings, so never accept a number from a sales sheet. [verify with product data sheet]

Why openings drive hurricane loss

"Because many structures were destroyed when door or window failures allowed wind pressure to demolish roofs from the interior, codes need to emphasize appropriate door and window storm covers."
National Weather Service, Hurricane Andrew Natural Disaster Survey Report

That finding is the origin of modern Florida glazing law. Hurricane Andrew produced roughly $25 billion in damage and destroyed 25,524 homes while damaging 101,241 more, according to the NOAA National Hurricane Center preliminary report. Florida's exposure is structural, not anecdotal: NOAA records 110 hurricane strikes on Florida between 1851 and 2004, including 35 major hurricanes of Category 3 or higher, with 41 strikes on southeast Florida alone, per the National Hurricane Center state strike tabulation.

Approvals

Florida Product Approval and Miami-Dade NOA Basics.

Florida has two parallel approval tracks, and a qualified commercial glazier works fluently in both.

  1. Statewide Florida Product Approval. Administered through the Florida Building Commission product approval system. Accepted in jurisdictions statewide unless a local authority requires more.
  2. Miami-Dade County Notice of Acceptance (NOA). Issued by Miami-Dade County Product Control, and expected in the HVHZ counties for exterior openings.

NOA testing runs on the Testing Application Standard protocols: TAS 201 is large missile impact, TAS 202 is air, water, and structural pressure, TAS 203 is cyclic wind pressure. Miami-Dade County publishes the sequencing directly: in Protocol TAS 202, if the product is impact resistant then TAS 201 and TAS 203 will also be performed, and one specimen to TAS 202 is allowed, while non-impact products require three units, per Miami-Dade County product testing guidance.

What belongs in a complete glazing submittal package

  • Cover sheet identifying project, address, jurisdiction, and HVHZ or WBDR status
  • Current approval or NOA for every installed system, with document number and expiration date [verify with product data sheet]
  • Shop drawings showing system selection, opening sizes, anchorage, and adjacent trade interfaces
  • Glass make-up specification that matches the approval listing exactly [verify with product data sheet]
  • Design pressure analysis tying the engineer's wind loads to the system rating, opening by opening [verify with product data sheet]
  • Signed and sealed engineering where a condition falls outside the tested configuration

Schedule

Planning the Glazing Package Backward From Enclosure.

Glazing is a long-lead, engineered package. The most reliable way to protect an enclosure date is to sequence the paperwork first and the glass second:

  1. Bid and scope review. Confirm inclusions, exclusions, alternates, and who owns perimeter waterproofing.
  2. Award and system confirmation. Lock the framing system against the required approval path before shop drawings start.
  3. Shop drawings. Produced, reviewed by the design team, and returned before fabrication release.
  4. Product approval and engineering package. Submitted with the permit set, not after.
  5. Material release and fabrication. Aluminum, glass fabrication, and hardware run on separate lead times. [verify with product data sheet]
  6. Field dimension verification. After the structural openings exist, before final fabrication where possible.
  7. Installation, glazing, and sealant. Sequenced against the waterproofing scope and inspection holds.
  8. Water testing, punch, and closeout. Warranties, as-builts, and approval documentation assembled for the owner.

A glazier who cannot state a lead time by system type has not priced the schedule risk.

Commercial vs Retail

Qualified Commercial Glazier vs Residential Glass Shop: Seven Differences.

Search results for glass companies in South Florida are dominated by retail glass shops. Many are good businesses, built for a different transaction. The differences that matter on a commercial job:

  • Licensing structure. Florida law defines a specialty contractor as one whose scope is limited to a particular phase of construction established by board rule, per Florida Statutes 489.105. Commercial glazing packages frequently require broader certified licensure.
  • Bonding. Retail glass shops generally carry no bonding capacity. Commercial subcontracts often require payment and performance bonds.
  • Insurance limits. Commercial subcontracts specify limits and additional insured status; retail work rarely does.
  • Shop drawing capability. Commercial glazing requires drafted, reviewable shop drawings and engineering coordination.
  • Approval documentation fluency. Permit reviewers reject incomplete NOA packages on first pass.
  • Schedule integration. Commercial glaziers work inside a CPM schedule with predecessors and inspection holds.
  • Closeout obligations. Warranties, as-builts, maintenance data, and approval records are contract deliverables.

ACG is commercial-only. The company does not sell shower doors, mirrors, residential window replacement, or auto glass. If that is what your project needs, a residential glass shop is the correct call and will serve you better on price and speed.

FAQ

Frequently Asked Questions.

Which commercial glazing contractors serve all of South Florida?

Look for a glazier with physical offices inside the region rather than a single location covering the whole state. American Commercial Glass operates from West Palm Beach with additional offices in Naples and Tampa, covering Palm Beach, Broward, Miami-Dade, Martin, St. Lucie, Collier, and Lee counties. Confirm coverage claims against the bidder's project list in each county you care about.

What is HVHZ and which South Florida counties are in it?

HVHZ is the High Velocity Hurricane Zone in the Florida Building Code, and it covers Miami-Dade and Broward counties. Inside HVHZ, exterior glazing must resist large missile impact within the lowest 30 feet of the building and small missile impact above that height, and fixed exterior glazing requires product approval.

Do I need a Miami-Dade NOA for a project in Palm Beach County?

Not automatically. Palm Beach County is in the Wind-Borne Debris Region rather than HVHZ, so statewide Florida Product Approval is generally the path. Some owners and design teams still specify NOA-listed systems, and some local amendments add requirements, so confirm with the authority having jurisdiction before pricing.

How long does a commercial glazing package take from award to installation?

Plan the sequence rather than a single number: scope review, award, shop drawings, design team review, product approval submittal, fabrication release, field verification, then installation. Lead times vary by framing system, glass make-up, and hardware, so ask each bidder for current written lead times by system type. [verify with product data sheet]

Can a residential glass company install commercial storefront or curtain wall?

Usually not appropriately. Commercial storefront and curtain wall packages require licensure suited to the scope, bonding capacity, insurance limits specified by the subcontract, shop drawing production, product approval documentation, and closeout deliverables. Retail glass shops are generally structured for direct-to-consumer replacement work instead.

What documentation should a glazing subcontractor hand over at closeout?

Expect manufacturer and installation warranties, as-built shop drawings, the approval or NOA documents used for permitting, glass and hardware maintenance data, and any water test reports. Missing closeout documentation is a common cause of held retainage on glazing scopes.
