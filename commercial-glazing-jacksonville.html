Duval County Code Path

Jacksonville Is Not HVHZ.
Do Not Submit an NOA.

The High Velocity Hurricane Zone is defined in the Florida Building Code as Miami-Dade and Broward counties only. Duval County is outside it. That single distinction drives the entire approval path for a Jacksonville glazing package, and getting it wrong is the most common reason a North Florida storefront submittal comes back rejected.

For Duval County work, the governing documents are statewide Florida Product Approval and the wind-load provisions of FBC Section 1609, which references ASCE 7 for design pressure calculation. A Miami-Dade Notice of Acceptance is not the required document here — it is a Miami-Dade County instrument. Systems tested under TAS 201/202/203 are tested to an HVHZ protocol; outside HVHZ, impact-rated products are approved against ASTM E1886 and E1996.

Parts of Duval and Nassau counties nearer the Atlantic fall inside the Wind-Borne Debris Region under FBC 1609.1.2, which triggers opening protection requirements. Whether a given site is inside the WBDR depends on the ultimate design wind speed at that address, so it is confirmed per project rather than assumed county-wide.

Procurement & Submittals

What the Paperwork
Actually Looks Like.

Glazing sits on the critical path to dry-in and carries the longest lead times in Division 08. On a Jacksonville project the sequence ACG runs is the same one we run statewide, with the Duval-specific approval path substituted in at bid stage rather than discovered at submittal stage.

  1. Bid package in. Architectural elevations, door and window schedules, glazing details, structural drawings, Division 08 specs (08 41 13, 08 44 13, 08 80 00), and the scheduled design pressures per opening.
  2. Scoped proposal back in five business days. Named manufacturer and system family, glass make-up, hardware, the specific Florida Product Approval number, schedule milestones, and value-engineering alternates where asked.
  3. Award and contract. AIA-format subcontract, certificates of insurance exchanged.
  4. Submittal package in two to three weeks. Shop drawings with head, jamb, and sill details; anchorage keyed to the actual substrate; the approval's design-pressure table shown against the scheduled pressures; glass performance values; hardware schedule; sealant and finish data.
  5. Fabrication, eight to fourteen weeks depending on system, glass make-up, and finish.
  6. Mobilization one week ahead. Rough opening tolerance verification per ASTM E2112, substrate check, crew briefing with your superintendent.
  7. Install with daily pre-task plans and weekly toolbox talks under OSHA 1926 Subpart M, sequenced against your master schedule.
  8. Field water testing per ASTM E1105 where the specification or owner requires it.
  9. Punch and closeout — warranty package, approval documentation, signed test reports, O&M handover.

RFI response is 48 hours standard and same-day when the question is holding up the field. ACG works in Procore and submits AIA G702/G703 pay applications. More detail: submittal process · shop drawings · what's in the package · Procore integration.

North Florida Market

Where the Glazing Work
Is in Northeast Florida.

Jacksonville is Florida's largest city by land area and one of its most varied commercial construction markets. Downtown redevelopment along the St. Johns River, healthcare campus expansion, and logistics construction driven by port and interstate access each generate a different glazing profile — and each puts different pressure on the submittal schedule.

Downtown and Southside mid-rise work tends toward curtainwall and window wall with structural coordination and long fabrication runs. Healthcare buildout brings fire-rated assemblies, interior partitions, and phased occupied-building sequencing. Retail and mixed-use along the Town Center corridor and through St. Johns County — Nocatee and the Ponte Vedra Beach commercial strip in particular — is predominantly ground-floor storefront with entrance hardware coordination.

Coastal Duval, Nassau, and eastern St. Johns sites are the ones most likely to fall inside the Wind-Borne Debris Region, which changes the opening-protection requirement and therefore the product selection. That is confirmed against the site's design wind speed during bid review, not assumed.
