What commercial storefront means in Miami (HVHZ)

Miami and Miami-Dade County sit at the origin of the Florida Building Code's High Velocity Hurricane Zone (HVHZ) designation. The HVHZ standard was created specifically in response to Hurricane Andrew's 1992 destruction of South Florida building stock - much of which was attributed to inadequate glazing and opening protection. Miami-Dade County's Product Control Section (part of the Department of Regulatory and Economic Resources) is the issuing authority for Notices of Acceptance (NOAs) - the mandatory approval document for every exterior glazing system installed in Miami-Dade HVHZ commercial buildings.

Commercial storefront in Miami is not simply aluminum framing with glass. In HVHZ Miami-Dade, every exterior storefront system must:

  • Carry a current Miami-Dade Product Control NOA identifying the specific tested configuration - frame profile, glass make-up, maximum frame size, design pressure ratings (positive and negative PSF), and anchorage specification.
  • Have been tested under TAS 201 (large-missile impact: 9-lb 2x4 at 50 ft/sec), TAS 202 (structural uniform air pressure to DP rating), and TAS 203 (9,000-cycle wind pressure cycling). All three tests must be passed for NOA issuance.
  • Be installed within the frame-size limitations of the NOA - openings larger than the tested size require an engineering letter of approval or a different system.
  • Be anchored to the substrate per the NOA-specified fastener type, spacing, and edge distance for the specific substrate (concrete, CMU, steel).

Miami-Dade NOA compliance is non-negotiable. Miami-Dade Building Department plan reviewers check NOA currency, NOA numbers against the RPED product control database, and glass make-up match against the NOA on every commercial storefront submittal. Incomplete packages are rejected without exception.

Miami's commercial construction market - Brickell, downtown, Wynwood, Edgewater, Design District, Coral Gables, Doral, Hialeah, and the northern corridor toward Aventura - represents one of the highest-volume HVHZ commercial glazing markets in North America. Retrofit projects, new construction, and tenant improvements all require the same NOA compliance pathway.

Code and permitting in Miami (Miami-Dade HVHZ)

Miami commercial storefront is governed by the Florida Building Code (FBC), 8th Edition, with HVHZ provisions under FBC Sections 1609 and 1709. Permits for the City of Miami are issued by the Miami Building Department; Miami-Dade County unincorporated projects are issued by the Miami-Dade County Building Department. HVHZ submittal requirements at both authorities:

  • Miami-Dade Product Control NOA - Required for every exterior glazing system. The NOA must be current at the time of permit submission, not simply at the time of system design or ordering. NOAs are public records in the RPED (Regulatory and Economic Resources Department) product control database, searchable by manufacturer and product line.
  • TAS 201 / 202 / 203 - Referenced in the NOA documentation. The submittal package must reference the specific TAS test reports by number and confirm that the proposed frame size and glass make-up are within the tested envelope.
  • ASCE 7 design pressure analysis - Required for every project. Miami's coastal exposure, building heights in Brickell and downtown, and HVHZ-specific wind speed requirements drive elevated DP demands that must be matched against the NOA's rated DP by frame size.
  • Glass make-up specification - Must match the NOA exactly. Miami-Dade plan reviewers cross-reference the glass specification in the shop drawings against the NOA-listed glass make-up on every submittal.
  • Anchorage details - Per the NOA. Miami's prevalent concrete, CMU, and steel substrates each have specific fastener requirements in the NOA documentation.
Miami-Dade Product Control - origin of HVHZ. Miami-Dade County's Product Control Section is the issuing authority for all HVHZ NOAs in Florida. The NOA database at miamidade.gov/building/pc-search.asp is the reference source for all current approvals. ACG verifies NOA currency on every Miami project at the time of permit submission and includes current NOA printouts in every submittal package.
