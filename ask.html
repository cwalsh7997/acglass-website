What's the difference between storefront and curtainwall?

Storefront systems are non-load-bearing aluminum frames typically used for ground-floor commercial entrances up to 10 feet tall. Curtainwall is engineered for multi-story buildings, carries its own dead load, and uses pressure-equalized rain screens. Specify storefront for retail, restaurants, and lobbies; specify curtainwall when you exceed two stories or need wind-rated facade performance.

What is impact-rated glazing?

Impact windows are commercial glazing assemblies that pass ASTM E1886 / E1996 large-missile impact testing — a 9-pound 2x4 fired at 50 ft/s, followed by 9,000+ pressure cycles. They eliminate the need for shutters in HVHZ counties (Miami-Dade, Broward), are required by code on Florida coastal commercial projects, and carry Florida Product Approvals or Miami-Dade NOAs unique to each system + glass + framing combination.

What does the Florida Building Code 8th Edition (2026) require for commercial glazing?

Florida 8th Edition (2026) is in effect. Key glazing impacts: ASCE 7-22 wind loads (10–15% higher in some counties vs prior edition), revised HVHZ test protocols, mandatory ANSI Z97.1 safety glazing in additional locations, and stricter sealant/water-test compliance for curtainwall.

Do I need a permit to replace commercial windows in Florida?

Yes — Florida requires a permit for almost all commercial window replacement. Like-for-like may qualify for a streamlined permit; any change in opening size, system type, glass thickness, or impact rating requires a full permit including signed/sealed engineering, Florida Product Approval submittals, and (in HVHZ) Miami-Dade NOA. Permitting timeline: 2–6 weeks typical, 6–12 weeks in HVHZ counties.

What's the difference between PGT and ESWindows for commercial Florida projects?

PGT is U.S.-manufactured (Venice, FL), strong on residential-spec impact lines, and dominates SW Florida residential. ESWindows is Colombian-engineered, dominates South Florida commercial high-rise (ES-7000, ES-8000, ES-50 storefront), and offers larger spans, better thermal performance, and tighter unitized assemblies. ACG installs both. For commercial you usually want ESWindows; for residential infill you usually want PGT.

Are 'impact windows' and 'hurricane windows' the same thing?

They're the same product. “Impact windows” and “hurricane windows” both refer to glazing that meets ASTM E1886 and E1996 large-missile impact testing. The Florida Building Code uses “impact-resistant.” What actually matters is the Florida Product Approval (NOA in Miami-Dade) and the design pressure rating for your specific opening size and HVHZ status.

Can vinyl windows work for Florida commercial projects?

For commercial Florida projects, aluminum is the right choice in 95% of cases. Vinyl frames soften above 160°F (Florida roof and west-facing facades regularly hit this), can't span large openings, and carry shorter Florida Product Approvals. Aluminum thermally-broken systems (ESWindows, PGT impact lines) handle HVHZ, larger spans, and 25+ year service life. Vinyl is residential-grade only.

What ADA compliance applies to commercial glass doors?

ADA-compliant commercial glass doors require: 32-inch minimum clear width, 5 lbs maximum opening force (interior, non-fire), 60×60-inch maneuvering clearances on push and pull sides, lever or panic hardware operable with one hand without tight grasping, and visible-marker stripes between 40–80 inches above finished floor on all-glass doors. Automatic operators reset the opening force requirement.

What types of commercial glass are specified in Florida?

The 8 commercial glass types specified in Florida: annealed, heat-strengthened (2× annealed strength), tempered (4×, breaks into small cubes — ANSI Z97.1 safety glazing), laminated (PVB or SGP interlayer — required for impact and overhead), insulating (IGU), low-E coated (solar control), spandrel (opaque), and fire-rated (TGP / Slimpact for 20–120 minute ratings). Most commercial impact assemblies use laminated tempered.

How often does Florida commercial glass need maintenance?

Florida commercial glass should be maintained on this cadence: exterior cleaning every 60–90 days (coastal) or 90–180 days (inland); perimeter sealant inspection annually with re-caulk every 8–12 years; structural silicone curtainwall inspection every 5 years; hurricane impact glass test of operable elements before May 1 each year. Logging maintenance preserves manufacturer warranty validity.

Do commercial windows block UV in Florida?

Standard tempered glass blocks ~25% of UV. Laminated impact glass blocks 99%+ (the PVB or SGP interlayer is the UV barrier). Low-E coated insulating units block 84–99% depending on coating. For Florida commercial buildings, almost all impact-rated assemblies already deliver near-total UV protection, which extends interior finishes 3–5× vs annealed glass.

Why does professional commercial glazing installation matter?

Florida Product Approvals are voided by deviation from tested anchor patterns and shim heights. Structural silicone curtainwall requires lab-trained applicators or the warranty voids. Impact-glazing failures during a storm event become a building-code negligence claim if installation didn't match the NOA.
