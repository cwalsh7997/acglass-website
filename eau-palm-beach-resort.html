The Resort

Eau Palm Beach is one of South Florida's most iconic oceanfront properties. Formerly the Ritz-Carlton Palm Beach, it rebranded in 2013 and has held a Forbes Five-Star rating for over a decade — one of the rarest distinctions in American hospitality. Set on seven private acres of Atlantic beachfront in Manalapan, it's the kind of property where every material decision is scrutinized, every finish is noticed, and nothing is left to chance.

The resort encompasses 309 guestrooms and suites, a 42,000-square-foot spa, multiple dining outlets including the signature Polpo restaurant, and seven acres of private beach. It's a member of Preferred Hotels & Resorts' Legend Collection — reserved for properties that represent the highest echelon of independent luxury. The guest who stays at Eau notices thread count. They notice lighting. They notice glass.

This is the context in which ACG worked.

The Scope

ACG was brought in to handle a multi-phase renovation across seven distinct areas of the resort. Working directly with ownership and resort management — no general contractor layer — this wasn't one scope. It was seven, each with its own design intent, timeline, and standard of execution. The phases ranged from signature statement pieces to the precise details that only professionals notice. All of it had to be executed inside an occupied, operating resort.

  • 01
    The New Front Door Custom decorative glass entrance doors — the resort's first impression. An elegant curved interlocking pattern set in white framing. The signature entry to Eau Palm Beach, visible from S Ocean Blvd, executed to the detail level the address demands.
  • 02
    Polpo Exterior Facade Wall The centerpiece of the renovation. A bi-fold opening glass wall system for Polpo restaurant that collapses the boundary between the dining room and the ocean terrace. When open, guests experience the Atlantic without interruption — palm trees, sea breeze, the full oceanfront panorama.
  • 03
    Polpo Interior Arched glass windows throughout the fine dining room — custom curved glass in arched frames. The chandeliers visible through the glass, the crisp white tablecloths, the Atlantic beyond: a dining room interior designed around what the glass frames and reveals.
  • 04
    Kids Club & Ice Cream Shop Floor-to-ceiling glazing for the resort's children's activity center and ice cream shop. Natural light and ocean views built into what could have been an interior space. The same standard of glass, applied to every corner of the property.
  • 05
    Spa Additions New glazing for the expansion of the resort's 42,000-square-foot spa — one of the largest resort spas in Florida. Glass for the new addition areas, maintaining the spa's connection to natural light and the Palm Beach landscape.
  • 06
    Rear Facade Entrances Updated glazing for the rear entry points of the resort. The back-of-house entrances that staff, service vehicles, and some guests use — held to the same standard as the front, because at a Forbes Five-Star property, there is no "back of house" standard.

"At a Forbes Five-Star property, the glazing standard isn't just 'meets code.' Every frame has to be aligned. Every seal invisible. Every pane crystal clear."

What Five-Star Means for Glass

A Forbes Five-Star rating isn't awarded — it's earned, and it's re-evaluated every year. The criteria cover hundreds of touch points: the response time on a guest request, the temperature of the towels, the condition of every surface in every space. Glass is everywhere in a resort. It's the lobby entry. It's the restaurant wall. It's the spa window. It's the view from the guestroom.

At this standard, glazing isn't infrastructure — it's design. Every frame has to be aligned. Every seal has to be invisible. Every pane has to be crystal clear, without distortion or imperfection. Guests at this level notice glass the way they notice thread count. They can't always name what they're seeing, but they feel it when it's right — and they definitely notice when it isn't.

ACG delivered to that standard across all seven phases. The installation had to happen around resort operations: around dinner service, around spa appointments, around guests checking in. The work had to be impeccable and invisible at the same time.

A Personal Project

Eau Palm Beach was one of ACG's earliest projects. Connor Walsh built this relationship from the ground up — directly with ownership and resort management — before ACG had a portfolio of hospitality projects to show. It was the project that proved something: that a lean, owner-operated glazing company could deliver at the highest level of hospitality construction in Palm Beach County.

The photo of Connor standing at the new front doors — the Eau monogram mat at his feet, the custom entrance he helped design and install behind him — represents that. Not just a completed scope. A relationship, a standard set, and a standard met.

For ACG, this project is the reference. When a luxury hotel or resort developer asks what we've done at this level, the answer is standing on the Atlantic coast in Manalapan, with a Forbes Five-Star rating and seven areas of custom glazing that have held up to the daily scrutiny of the most demanding guests in the country.
