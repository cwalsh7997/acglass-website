WaterColor commercial glazing questions, answered.

Who reviews WaterColor commercial submittals?

WaterColor's Architectural Review Committee (ARC) reviews all commercial alterations, additions, and new builds inside the master-planned community. Our submittals include profile drawings, finish samples, and divided-lite layouts to clear ARC on first pass.

Is St. Joe Company the developer?

Yes. St. Joe Company developed and continues to control commercial at WaterColor. Most large GC contracts are with St. Joe or St. Joe affiliates.

Is impact glazing required at WaterColor?

Yes. Coastal Walton County is full WBDR — 135 mph design wind, Florida Product Approval impact certification required on every commercial window and storefront.

Can you handle Watersound, Watersound Origins, and 30Avenue from a WaterColor project?

Yes. The St. Joe portfolio across WaterColor, WaterSound, Watersound Origins, and 30Avenue is inside one operational service zone for us. We coordinate scope across multiple St. Joe assets when the GC has work in more than one.

What's a typical WaterColor commercial storefront package look like?

Restaurant: 25–60 LF of impact-rated narrow-sightline storefront, often with a Eurowall folding or sliding Gulf-facing assembly, dark bronze anodize finish, transom with applied muntin pattern, oil-rubbed bronze hardware. Retail: 30–80 LF of impact storefront with full-light entrance doors. Typical packages run $80–250K.
