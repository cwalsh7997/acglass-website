The Panama City Market

Post-Michael Rebuild and a
Market Built to Last.

Hurricane Michael made landfall near Mexico Beach on October 10, 2018, as one of the most powerful storms ever to hit the continental United States - and the most destructive hurricane to strike the Florida Panhandle on record. The Category 5 storm devastated Panama City, Mexico Beach, Lynn Haven, Springfield, and surrounding communities, destroying or severely damaging billions of dollars in commercial, institutional, and residential structures.

That devastation created one of the most sustained commercial reconstruction programs in Florida history - and it continues today. The Panama City and Bay County rebuild is not a sprint. It is a multi-year, multi-billion-dollar reconstruction program that is actively reshaping the built environment of Northwest Florida's Gulf Coast. GCs who work this market need glazing subs who understand the rebuild dynamic: accelerated timelines, insurance documentation requirements, and the absolute necessity of properly specified impact-rated systems that will survive the next major storm event.

Tyndall Air Force Base - one of the most significant military construction projects in the Southeast following Michael - is rebuilding essentially its entire facility footprint. The Bay Medical Center campus serves the region's healthcare construction demand. Panama City Beach's hotel corridor along Front Beach Road, the Pier Park commercial complex, and the expanding Breakfast Point commercial district represent the private-sector commercial pipeline. Mexico Beach and Port St. Joe are rebuilding Gulf County's coastal communities.

Every commercial glazing project in Bay County requires full coastal high-wind-zone impact compliance. Following Michael, wind speed mapping for Bay County was updated to reflect the extreme wind event that struck the Panhandle. ACG engineers every submittal to the current design wind pressure, with verified Florida product approvals built for post-Michael Bay County plan review standards.

ACG's statewide reach extends to Panama City via logistics - the same scope depth and 48-hour responsiveness that GCs rely on across Florida, applied to the Emerald Coast's most active and demanding reconstruction market.

Common Questions

Panama City Glazing FAQ.

Does ACG have experience with post-Hurricane Michael rebuild projects? +

Yes. Hurricane Michael created among the most extensive multi-year commercial reconstruction programs in Florida history. ACG understands the rebuild dynamic - accelerated schedules, insurance documentation requirements, and the critical importance of properly specified, tested, and installed impact-rated systems that will survive the next major storm event. Our logistics are built for the schedule pressure of post-disaster rebuild projects.

What impact glazing requirements apply in Panama City and Bay County? +

Bay County is in a coastal high-wind zone under Florida Building Code - impact-rated glazing is required throughout. Following Hurricane Michael, Bay County's wind speed designations were updated to reflect the extreme wind event that struck the Panhandle. ACG engineers every submittal to the current design wind pressure for Bay County, with verified Florida product approvals and NOA documentation built for post-Michael plan review standards. No substitutions. No product approval surprises during review.

Does ACG serve the statewide market including Panama City? +

Yes. ACG operates statewide through logistics - 3 Florida offices covering West Palm Beach, Naples, and Tampa, with statewide reach to the Panhandle and North Florida. Our coordinated scheduling, submittals, and procurement extend the full ACG service model to Panama City, Bay County, and Gulf County without requiring a permanent local office. 48-hour scope turnaround, no site visit required.

What glazing systems does ACG install in Panama City projects? +

ACG installs ESWindows and Euro-Wall impact windows and doors; PGT aluminum storefront; Euro-Wall curtainwall and window wall; Slimpact; TGP fire-rated glass assemblies; Allegion automatic entrance hardware; and Aldora architectural glass. All systems carry verified Florida product approvals for Bay County's coastal wind zone - specified to the project, not substituted for convenience.

How quickly can ACG turn around a scope for a Panama City project? +

Send us your drawings and we return a detailed scope - system recommendations, quantities, and Division 08 scope breakdown - within 48 hours. Our bid process reviews plans digitally and applies current Bay County code requirements, including post-Michael wind zone designations. No site visit required.
