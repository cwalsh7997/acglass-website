Florida & Federal Commercial Glazing — Standards Reference (2026).

Consolidated technical reference for three domains that govern commercial glazing on Florida projects and federal construction: HVHZ wind design pressures under FBC 2023, DoD AT/FP blast-rated glazing under UFC 4-010-01, and TAA / Buy American manufacturer sourcing under FAR Part 25.

Compiled by American Commercial Glass · FL CGC #1531993 Author Connor Walsh Last updated September 1, 2026 Scope FL statewide + federal Div 08

Last updated: September 1, 2026. This is a spec-writer's reference. Every table cell is traceable to a cited primary source. Design pressures and standoff distances are project-specific — the project structural engineer and, on AT/FP work, the project blast consultant, remain authoritative for a given building.

1. Florida HVHZ design-pressure reference — FBC 2023 Section 1620

The Florida High-Velocity Hurricane Zone is defined in Florida Building Code, Building (2023, 8th Edition) Section 202 as Miami-Dade County and Broward County only. HVHZ wind loads are computed per FBC-B Section 1620 using ASCE 7-22 Chapters 26 through 31, with 3-second gust design wind speeds fixed by Risk Category and county.

Per the 2023 Florida Building Code, Building — Chapter 16 (ICC Digital Codes), HVHZ wind design follows Section 1620 and its subsections. Section 1620.1 requires buildings to meet the requirements of ASCE 7 Chapters 26 through 31 (with tornado provisions per Chapter 32 for Risk Category III and IV per Section 1620.7). Section 1620.2 fixes the 3-second gust design wind speed by county and Risk Category; Section 1620.3 defaults all HVHZ buildings to Exposure Category C unless Exposure D applies under ASCE 7 Section 26.7. Outside HVHZ, Section 1609 governs and design wind speeds are read from FBC-B Figures 1609.3(1) through 1609.3(4) or the ASCE 7-22 Hazard Tool.

Product approval inside HVHZ is documented on a Miami-Dade County Notice of Acceptance (NOA) issued by the county Product Control Section, following the Testing Application Standards: TAS 201 (large- and small-missile impact), TAS 202 (uniform static air pressure), and TAS 203 (cyclic wind pressure loading). Outside HVHZ, the Florida Product Approval (FL#) system administered by the Florida Building Commission is the accepted pathway; products approved for HVHZ use may carry both an NOA and an HVHZ-designated FL#.

2. AT/FP blast-rated glazing reference — UFC 4-010-01 and ASTM F-series

The Department of Defense minimum antiterrorism standard for buildings is UFC 4-010-01, which prescribes standoff distances and requires that windows in inhabited DoD buildings meet a hazard rating no worse than that produced by the standoff and applicable blast load. Blast-rated glazing is tested to ASTM F1642, designed to ASTM F2248 for laminated assemblies, and specified as an assembly under ASTM F2912.

The Whole Building Design Guide's UFC 4-010-01 portal (WBDG, current 2026 revision) hosts the active DoD Minimum Antiterrorism Standards for Buildings. UFC 4-010-01 mandates conventional-construction standoff distances (measured from an installation's controlled perimeter and from a parking / roadway to the nearest inhabited building) below which additional analysis, hardening, or hazard mitigation is required. On glazing specifically, UFC 4-010-01 requires that windows in inhabited buildings not exceed a specified hazard rating under ASTM F1642 for the calculated blast load at the actual standoff.

The ASTM airblast glazing suite works together. Per ASTM F1642/F1642M-17, the test method establishes hazard rating tiers based on post-test debris location: No Break, No Hazard, Minimal Hazard, Very Low Hazard, Low Hazard, Medium Hazard, and High Hazard. Per ASTM F2248-19, the practice provides an equivalent 3-second duration design load that a structural engineer can pair with the glass thickness design procedure of ASTM E1300 to size laminated blast-resistant glazing without a full dynamic analysis. Per ASTM F2912-17, the specification is the assembly-level standard: it covers glazing, glazed windows, glazed curtain walls, and glazed protective systems subjected to intentional or accidental airblast, tying the hazard rating from F1642 to the installed system's frame anchorage and glazing bite.

3. TAA and Buy American reference — federal construction glazing sourcing

On federal construction contracts, glazing manufacturer sourcing is governed by two distinct regimes: the Buy American Act (as implemented in FAR Part 25, Subpart 25.2 for construction materials) and the Trade Agreements Act (19 U.S.C. § 2511, as implemented in FAR Subpart 25.4 and FAR clause 52.225-5). The applicable regime is a function of contract value, agency, and whether the acquisition is TAA-covered — a determination made by the contracting officer, not the subcontractor.

Per FAR Part 25 — Foreign Acquisition (Acquisition.gov), subpart 25.2 requires that only domestic construction materials be used on covered federal construction contracts performed in the United States, subject to exceptions in FAR 25.202. The Buy American Act's two-part test for a domestic manufactured end product requires U.S. manufacture and, for 2024–2028 deliveries, that the cost of domestic components exceed 65 percent of the cost of all components (rising to 75 percent in 2029). Subpart 25.4 implements the Trade Agreements Act: on TAA-covered acquisitions, end products and construction materials from TAA-designated countries receive equal treatment with domestic offers.

The current TAA designated-country list is published by GSA at GSA — Look up Trade Agreements Act-designated countries and mirrors FAR 52.225-5. It comprises WTO Government Procurement Agreement signatories, Free Trade Agreement partners (including CAFTA-DR and USMCA countries), designated Caribbean Basin countries, and Least Developed Countries. Colombia, Canada, the United Kingdom, Japan, Korea, Germany, and Italy are all TAA-designated; China, India, Brazil, Russia, Malaysia, and Vietnam are not.
