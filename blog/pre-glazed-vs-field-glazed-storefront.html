Definitions: pre-glazed vs field-glazed

Pre-glazed (factory-glazed) means glass is set into the framing at the manufacturing plant. Units ship as glazed assemblies. Field crews set the unit, anchor it, apply perimeter seals, and set hardware.

Field-glazed means frames and glass travel as separate packages (or on staggered releases). Crews erect framing, then set glass on site with setting blocks, stops or structural silicone as designed, and weatherseals.

Both methods can use the same storefront system family. The choice is where the glaze labor and glass risk live: plant or jobsite. Factory QA

Pre-glazed moves glaze inspection into a controlled plant. Bite, gasket seating, and unit squareness get checked before freight. That does not remove field responsibility for anchors, perimeter seals, and opening tolerances.

Field-glazed leaves glaze QA on the scaffold or swing stage. Frame can still be factory-cut and finished. The quality of the installed lite depends on blocks, stops or structural silicone detailing, and weather during cure.

Glass handling risk

Pre-glazed units protect the lite edges inside the frame, but a tip-over or fork strike often condemns the whole assembly. Replacement lead time follows the full unit, not a single lite.

Field-glazed glass needs dedicated racks, corner protection, and a clear path from truck to floor. More touch points increase scratch and break risk. Frames can go up while glass is still in fabrication, which can help the envelope schedule if openings are ready.

Site labor hour drivers

Count the tasks, not a marketing “percent less labor” claim.

  • Pre-glazed drivers: unit set, shim and plumb, anchor, perimeter sealant, hardware, clean-down.
  • Field-glazed drivers: frame set and anchor, plus a second pass for glass: setting blocks, stops or SSG, temporary protection, weatherseals, clean-down.

Large modules, multi-lite ribbons, and limited swing-stage time change which column wins. Run the worksheet on the actual elevation, not a generic storefront paragraph.

Crane and access

Ask three site questions before you pick a method:

  1. Can the heaviest glazed unit land within crane or forklift reach without crossing finished work?
  2. Is there dry indoor staging for glazed modules, or only outdoor laydown?
  3. Can glass racks roll to the opening if you field-glaze?

Tight urban sites and upper floors often force one answer. Do not assume pre-glazed is always faster if the crane day is the bottleneck.

Weather, schedule float, and punch

Weather: Florida afternoon storms punish open glaze work. Pre-glazed can close an opening once the unit is set and perimeter-sealed. Field glaze needs a dry window for the glass and sealant pass.

Schedule float: Pre-glazed wants dimensions locked earlier because glass and frame travel together. Field-glazed can stagger releases: frames first, glass later. Late architectural changes hit differently under each path.

Punch risk: Pre-glazed punch leans toward unit defects and set/seal issues. Field-glazed punch adds lite scratches, incorrect bite, and sealant workmanship. Water test requirements (when the spec calls for them) still apply to both methods.

How to choose on a Florida GC schedule

Choose pre-glazed when openings are locked, crane access is real, and you want fewer weather-dependent glaze days. Choose field-glazed when sizes may move, when glazed unit weight exceeds site lift capacity, or when frames must go early and glass can follow.

Many projects mix methods by elevation. Put the method note on the opening schedule so buyout and freight match.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is pre-glazed storefront?

Pre-glazed (factory-glazed) storefront arrives with glass already set in the frame at the plant. Field work focuses on setting framed units, anchoring, perimeter sealant, and hardware, not cutting and setting glass lite by lite on the scaffold.

What is field-glazed storefront?

Field-glazed storefront ships frames and glass separately (or frames first, glass later). Crews assemble and glaze on site. That adds glass handling, setting-block work, and weather exposure during the glaze pass.

Does pre-glazed always cost less?

No. Total installed cost depends on unit size, access, freight, damage risk, and how many openings the schedule can absorb in one set day. This page does not publish savings percentages. Run both methods against the same opening schedule.

When does field glazing win?

Field glazing often wins when openings need late size confirmation, when freight or crane capacity cannot move glazed units, or when the site can stage frames early and glaze after the envelope is more protected.
