How Much Does Curtainwall
Cost Per Square Foot?

Real numbers for 2026. Stick-built vs unitized, Florida HVHZ premiums, and what drives your price up or down.

Connor Walsh · 2026-06-10 · 8 min read
Quick Answer

Curtainwall costs between $150 and $350+ per square foot installed in 2026. Florida projects run higher — especially in the High Velocity Hurricane Zone (HVHZ). Here's what drives that number and how to budget accurately.

The Quick Answer: Curtainwall Cost Ranges

There is no single number for curtainwall cost. It depends on the system type, building height, glass selection, wind requirements, and your location. But here are realistic ranges for most Florida commercial projects:

System Type Cost Range (Installed) Best For
Stick-built, standard $150–$220/SF Low-rise, budget projects, smaller facades
Stick-built, Florida HVHZ $200–$280/SF Miami-Dade/Broward impact-rated projects
Unitized, standard $220–$300/SF Mid-rise and high-rise, repetitive bays
Unitized, Florida HVHZ $260–$380/SF High-rise in Miami-Dade/Broward
Custom / specialty $300–$500+/SF Complex geometry, custom profiles, high-end facades

These are fully installed costs — materials, fabrication, labor, engineering, and glazing. They do not include structural steel embeds, HVAC integration, or facade engineering fees.

Stick-Built vs Unitized: What's the Difference?

Stick-Built Curtainwall

Stick-built curtainwall is assembled on the building, one piece at a time. Installers attach horizontal and vertical framing members (called "sticks") to the structure, then set glass or spandrel panels into the frames.

Stick-built costs less in material but more in labor. Each joint is made in the field, which takes time — especially on tall buildings where scaffold time is expensive. Quality depends heavily on installer skill.

Stick-built makes sense for:

  • Low-rise buildings (under 6 stories)
  • Projects with irregular bay spacing
  • Renovation work where access is limited
  • Smaller facade areas (under 5,000 SF)

Unitized Curtainwall

Unitized curtainwall is factory-assembled in panels. Each panel arrives at the job site pre-glazed and ready to hang. Panels stack on each other and interlock — installation is much faster than stick-built.

The panels cost more to fabricate. But on tall buildings, the reduced scaffold time and faster installation cycle often make unitized more cost-efficient overall. A 20-story building with unitized curtainwall can be glazed in weeks. The same facade in stick-built could take months.

Unitized makes sense for:

  • High-rise projects (6+ stories)
  • Repetitive, regular bay patterns
  • Projects with tight schedules
  • Large facade areas (10,000+ SF)

What Drives Curtainwall Cost Up

1. Building Height

Every additional story adds cost. Higher buildings require more wind pressure design, more robust anchors, and more complex installation logistics. Scaffold, crane time, and safety requirements all increase with height. A 20-story curtainwall project can cost 30–50% more per square foot than a 4-story project with the same system.

2. Florida Wind Load Requirements

Florida has the most demanding wind load requirements in the continental U.S. Buildings in Wind Zone 4 (coastal areas) and the HVHZ require systems engineered for 150+ mph wind speeds. This means heavier extrusions, more anchors, and impact-rated glass — all of which add cost.

The HVHZ premium is real. A curtainwall system that costs $200/SF in Tampa may cost $260/SF for the same project in Miami-Dade with NOA-approved impact glass.

3. Glass Selection

Glass is typically 40–60% of curtainwall cost. Your choices matter:

  • Standard IGU (insulating glass unit): Baseline cost
  • Low-E coated IGU: Adds $8–$15/SF but reduces HVAC loads significantly
  • Impact-rated laminated glass: Adds $15–$30/SF over standard IGU
  • Electrochromic or fritted glass: Can double the glass cost
  • Spandrel glass: Covers floor slabs — typically less expensive than vision glass

4. Custom Profiles and Finishes

Standard curtainwall comes in mill finish aluminum or two-coat painted finishes (Kynar 500). Custom colors, three-coat finishes, or anodized aluminum add $10–$25/SF. Custom profile shapes or non-standard sightlines require custom extrusion dies, which add $20,000–$80,000 in upfront tooling cost amortized across the project.

5. Thermal Performance Requirements

Standard aluminum curtainwall is a thermal bridge. A thermal break — a plastic or composite insert that separates the interior and exterior frame — adds $15–$30/SF but dramatically improves energy performance. Florida energy code (ASHRAE 90.1) requires thermal performance criteria that most projects in South Florida now meet with thermally broken systems.

Florida-Specific Cost Factors

Florida projects face several cost drivers that don't apply in other states:

  • HVHZ compliance: Miami-Dade NOA required for all glazing products in Miami-Dade and Broward counties. Systems without NOA cannot be permitted. NOA-approved systems cost more to fabricate and test.
  • Impact glass everywhere: Even outside HVHZ, Florida's wind zone map means most coastal commercial buildings need impact-rated glass. Impact laminated glass is standard on most Florida curtainwall projects.
  • Salt air exposure: Coastal projects need premium sealants, aluminum with better anodizing, and stainless fasteners to resist corrosion. This adds $8–$15/SF on oceanfront buildings.
  • Thermal movement: Florida's heat drives significant thermal expansion and contraction. Sealant systems and joint details must accommodate this movement — especially on west and south facades.

How to Get an Accurate Curtainwall Budget Number

The most accurate budget comes from sending plans to a qualified curtainwall contractor early. A good contractor can give you a detailed estimate — not just a per-SF number — that accounts for your specific conditions.

When budgeting early (before design development), use these rules of thumb:

  • Standard Florida project, 4–6 stories: $200–$250/SF
  • High-rise, 10+ stories, South Florida: $280–$350/SF
  • HVHZ project with custom profiles: $320–$450/SF
  • Add 10–15% contingency for bidding market volatility

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the average curtainwall cost per square foot in Florida?

In Florida, curtainwall typically costs $180–$350+ per square foot installed. Standard stick-built systems run $180–$250/SF. Unitized systems run $220–$350/SF. HVHZ projects in Miami-Dade or Broward run $260–$380/SF for unitized. Complex geometry and custom profiles can push costs above $400/SF.

What is the difference between stick-built and unitized curtainwall cost?

Stick-built has lower material cost but higher labor cost, especially on tall buildings. Unitized costs more to fabricate but installs faster and with fewer field errors. On projects over 6 stories, unitized often costs less overall because of reduced scaffold time. On smaller or irregular projects, stick-built is usually more cost-efficient.

Does curtainwall cost more in Florida than other states?

Yes — typically 20–40% more. Florida's strict wind load requirements, HVHZ impact glass mandates, salt air exposure, and thermal performance standards all add cost. Projects in Miami-Dade and Broward require Miami-Dade NOA-approved systems, which are more expensive to fabricate and test than standard systems used elsewhere.
