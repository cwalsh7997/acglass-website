Cost of Impact Windows for Commercial
Buildings in Florida (2026 Pricing)

Per-SF installed rates, HVHZ premiums, project size examples, and the variables that move the number up or down — for commercial buyers who need real figures.

Connor Walsh, ACG · 2026-04-22 · 11 min read

The cost of impact windows for commercial buildings in Florida is one of the most searched — and most misquoted — figures in the commercial glazing industry. Most pricing you find online is residential: per-unit figures for single-family replacement windows that have almost no bearing on what a commercial storefront, window wall, or curtainwall project actually costs. This guide covers 2026 commercial impact window pricing in Florida the way glazing contractors actually price it: per square foot of glass area, by system type, with HVHZ adjustments and real project examples you can use for early budgeting.

Commercial Impact Window Costs at a Glance

Before getting into the detail, here is where commercial impact window costs land in 2026 Florida — installed, including materials, labor, flashing, sealants, and disposal:

  • Impact storefront: $65–$120/SF installed, typical midpoint $85/SF
  • Window wall impact: $75–$140/SF installed, typical midpoint $100/SF
  • Curtainwall impact: $90–$180/SF installed, typical midpoint $125/SF
  • Impact IGU swapout in existing frame: $45–$85/SF
  • Impact door systems: $800–$2,800 per door unit
  • HVHZ (Miami-Dade / Broward) adds 15–25% to any of the above

These are commercial figures — not residential. The per-window pricing commonly cited for homeowner replacement projects ($400–$1,200 per window) does not translate to commercial work, where opening sizes, structural requirements, system complexity, and installation conditions are fundamentally different.

How Impact Windows Are Priced (Per SF, Not Per Window)

Commercial glazing contractors price by the square foot of glass area — the total of all opening dimensions across the project. This is the correct unit for commercial work because commercial openings vary enormously in size: a single curtainwall bay might be 10 feet wide and 12 feet tall, while a standard office window might be 4 feet by 5 feet. Pricing by the unit obscures this variation and leads to budget errors that surface late in a project.

The per-SF figure covers four cost components:

  • Glass and framing materials — the impact-rated system itself, including laminated glass fabrication, aluminum frame extrusions, hardware, and anchors
  • Glazier labor — installation time for the frame, glass, and perimeter detail work
  • Access and equipment — crane or aerial lift rentals for upper-floor work, scaffolding where required
  • Flashing, sealants, and incidentals — perimeter sealant, backer rod, flashing membrane, and debris disposal

When comparing quotes, confirm that all four components are included. A material-only quote or a quote that excludes crane time on a multi-story project will look lower-priced but produce cost surprises at billing.

HVHZ Premium: What Miami-Dade and Broward Projects Pay Extra

Florida's High Velocity Hurricane Zone covers Miami-Dade and Broward counties. Projects in the HVHZ operate under more demanding requirements than the rest of the state, and those requirements produce a measurable cost premium at every project size.

Zone Premium Why
Non-HVHZ FloridaBaselineFlorida Building Code (FBC) approval required
HVHZ (Miami-Dade, Broward)+15–25%NOA required, TAS 201/202/203 testing, enhanced field inspection

The premium has three sources. First, not every impact-rated product available elsewhere in Florida carries Miami-Dade Notice of Acceptance (NOA). The approved product list for HVHZ is shorter, which reduces competitive pressure and raises material cost. Second, HVHZ testing requirements under TAS 201 (large missile impact), TAS 202 (uniform static air pressure), and TAS 203 (cyclic wind pressure loading) are more rigorous than standard FBC testing — products meeting that standard cost more to manufacture. Third, field inspection requirements in HVHZ are more frequent and detailed, adding to project soft costs.

For projects in Palm Beach County and northward, HVHZ requirements do not apply. For projects spanning county lines, apply HVHZ pricing to the portions in Miami-Dade or Broward. What Drives Impact Window Cost Up or Down

Within the ranges above, several variables move the final installed price. Understanding them lets you estimate where your project falls before engaging a glazing contractor for formal pricing.

System Type and Structural Complexity

Storefront is the most cost-efficient impact system for commercial applications — lower-priced material, simpler installation, and wide product availability. Window wall and curtainwall systems involve more complex structural connections, larger glass lites, and more precise installation tolerances that increase both material and labor cost. If your project can use storefront where it was previously specified as window wall, there is typically a meaningful cost reduction available.

Glass Specification

The baseline impact glass specification for most commercial Florida projects is 1-inch laminated impact IGU (two lites of tempered or heat-strengthened glass with a structural interlayer, in an insulating unit). Upgrading to triple-silver low-E coatings, higher-performance SHGC configurations, or specialty tints adds $8–$20/SF to the glass cost alone. For large projects, specifying the right glass performance level — rather than over-specifying — is one of the most productive ways to manage total cost.

Floor Height and Access

Ground-floor work requires minimal equipment beyond standard glazier tools. Work above the second floor typically requires aerial lifts or crane picks, which add $5–$15/SF depending on floor height, urban site constraints, and the number of mobilizations required. High-rise curtainwall work in dense urban markets carries the highest access cost.

Pre-Glazed vs. Field-Glazed Storefront

Pre-glazed storefront systems — where the glass is installed into the frame at the factory before shipment to the job site — can reduce total installed cost compared to field-glazed systems on larger projects. Factory glazing is more controlled and faster than field installation, reducing glazier labor hours per unit and lowering the risk of field sealant defects. ESWindows pre-glazed storefront systems are one example of this approach available in the Florida market. On a 2,000–5,000 SF storefront replacement, the labor savings from pre-glazing can offset the logistics coordination cost and produce a lower total number than field-glazed alternatives.

Project Size

Volume drives material efficiencies. A 500 SF storefront replacement carries higher per-SF cost than a 5,000 SF replacement from the same contractor using the same system, because mobilization, shop drawing, and project management costs are spread over more square footage. Multi-site rollouts — retail chains, multifamily portfolios, office park upgrades — can produce additional savings through coordinated procurement and schedule efficiency.

Scope: New Installation vs. Replacement

Replacement work adds demolition and removal cost that new construction does not carry. Removing existing aluminum framing, hauling debris, and patching any substrate damage typically adds $5–$12/SF to the effective cost of a replacement project vs. a new-construction scope of the same system type.

Cost Comparison: Impact Windows vs Hurricane Shutters vs Standard

Commercial building owners in Florida typically weigh three envelope strategies: impact glazing, hurricane shutters over non-impact glazing, or standard non-impact glazing with accepted storm risk. The initial capital cost comparison favors shutters or standard glazing, but the full-cycle cost comparison is more nuanced.

Hurricane shutters on a commercial building carry recurring costs that are easy to undercount: annual deployment and retrieval labor (significant on a large building), storage space requirements, maintenance and replacement of hardware, and the operational disruption of closing a commercial building for shutter installation before a storm. On a 10,000 SF commercial building, the annual operational cost of a shutter program can run $15,000–$40,000 per year in labor and maintenance — costs that do not appear in the initial capital comparison but compound over the hold period.

Impact glazing eliminates the shutter program, qualifies the building for wind mitigation insurance credits (typically 10–20% reduction in wind premium for Florida commercial), improves energy performance through low-E IGU options, and enhances tenant experience. For commercial properties where the building is held as income property, the long-term economics of impact glazing over shutters are typically favorable within five to eight years of installation.

Labor and Installation Cost Breakdown

Labor typically accounts for 35–50% of total installed cost on a commercial impact window project, varying by system complexity and site conditions. Breaking down what that labor covers:

Glazier Labor

Skilled glazier installation is the core labor cost — setting frames, installing glass, applying wet seals, and completing hardware and trim. Florida commercial glaziers working on impact systems typically run $85–$130/hour depending on market and certification requirements. A standard ground-floor storefront replacement runs 0.6–1.2 glazier hours per square foot of glass area.

Crane and Aerial Lift Rentals

Upper-floor work requires either a crane (for large or heavy glass lites) or aerial work platform (for smaller units). Crane mobilization in Florida urban markets runs $2,500–$6,000 per day including operator. Aerial lifts run $400–$900/day. Projects requiring multiple crane picks for large curtainwall lites can add $20,000–$80,000 in equipment cost to a mid-size project — a significant line item that should be explicit in any quote.

Flashing, Sealants, and Waterproofing

Perimeter sealant and flashing are not cosmetic — they are the primary water management system for the glazing assembly. Commercial impact window installations require high-performance sealant (typically silicone or polyurethane, project-specific), backer rod, and in many cases flashing membrane at the head and sill. These materials and the labor to install them correctly run $3–$8/SF on most commercial projects.

Debris Removal and Disposal

Glass and aluminum demolition waste from replacement projects is hauled and disposed of by the glazing contractor. Disposal cost typically runs $1–$3/SF depending on project size and proximity to disposal facilities.

How to Budget Impact Windows for Your Project

For early-stage budgeting before formal quotes are in hand, a reliable approach is to take the total glass area of the project (all opening dimensions measured) and apply the typical per-SF figure for the most likely system type. Use the midpoint of the range unless you have a reason to apply the high or low end based on the variables described above. Add 15–25% if the project is in Miami-Dade or Broward.

For a 3,000 SF impact storefront replacement in Palm Beach County: 3,000 SF × $85/SF = $255,000 base estimate. That is a reasonable owner's budget number for planning purposes. The formal quote from a licensed glazing contractor will sharpen that number based on actual field conditions, system specification, and current material pricing.

A few budget disciplines that consistently improve accuracy:

  • Measure actual glass area, not wall area. Opening dimensions only.
  • Identify all floors above grade that require lift or crane access before estimating.
  • Confirm whether the project is new installation or replacement — add $5–$12/SF for demo and removal if replacing existing systems.
  • Flag HVHZ status early. It affects product availability, lead times, and pricing from the first vendor call.
  • Get multiple quotes from CGC-licensed glazing subcontractors. Florida law requires a CGC or equivalent license for commercial glazing work. ACG's license is CGC #1531993.

The Bottom Line: What to Expect

The cost of impact windows for a commercial building in Florida in 2026 is a function of system type, glass specification, floor height, HVHZ status, and project size — not a single number. The per-SF framework is the correct tool for commercial budgeting, and the ranges above reflect what ACG sees on real Florida commercial projects at current material and labor rates.

A few things that hold consistently across project types:

  • Impact storefront is the most cost-efficient system for most commercial applications
  • HVHZ adds real cost — budget for it upfront rather than discovering it at bid time
  • Pre-glazed systems like ESWindows can reduce installed cost on larger projects vs. field-glazed alternatives
  • The long-term economics of impact glazing over hurricane shutters are favorable for commercial income properties within five to eight years
  • Per-window residential pricing has no bearing on commercial scopes — use per-SF figures for commercial budgeting
