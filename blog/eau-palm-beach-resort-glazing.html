Behind the Glass at Eau Palm Beach —
A Forbes Five-Star Renovation

Connor Walsh · February 4, 2026 · 7 min read · Luxury Resort Glazing, Palm Beach County

The Scene at Polpo

Picture it at dusk. You're seated at Polpo, the signature restaurant at Eau Palm Beach Resort & Spa in Manalapan. The linen is crisp. The light is warm. And the wall — the entire wall facing the Atlantic — is simply open. No barrier. No screen. Just the ocean breeze moving through the dining room, palm trees catching the last of the sun, the sound of waves from the beach below the terrace.

What creates that moment isn't the menu. It isn't the chandeliers or the white tablecloths or the arched windows framing the views from inside the dining room. It's the glass — specifically, a bi-fold opening glass wall system that, when deployed, disappears entirely and lets the Atlantic in.

That glass wall was designed, sourced, and installed by a commercial glazing contractor out of West Palm Beach. The contractor is American Commercial Glass. The moment it creates, night after night, for guests at one of Florida's most iconic five-star properties — that's the work.

What Eau Palm Beach Is

Eau Palm Beach Resort & Spa sits on seven acres of private oceanfront in Manalapan, Florida — the exclusive town just south of Palm Beach proper, on a narrow barrier island where the Atlantic is on one side and the Intracoastal is on the other. The address is 100 South Ocean Boulevard. The property was formerly the Ritz-Carlton Palm Beach before rebranding as Eau in 2013.

It has held a Forbes Five-Star rating for over a decade. That puts it in rare company — there are fewer than 200 Forbes Five-Star hotels in the United States. It's a member of Preferred Hotels & Resorts' Legend Collection, reserved for the highest echelon of independent luxury properties worldwide. The spa is 42,000 square feet — one of the largest resort spas in Florida. None of this is background. It's context for what the glass had to be.

The Renovation: Seven Phases

ACG was brought in directly by ownership and resort management — no general contractor layer — to handle a multi-phase glazing renovation across seven distinct areas of the property. Here's what the scope actually covered:

  • 01
    The New Front Door Custom decorative glass entrance doors with an elegant curved interlocking pattern set in white framing. The first thing guests touch when they arrive. The signature entry to the resort.
  • 02
    Polpo Exterior Facade Wall The bi-fold opening glass wall that lets Polpo breathe into the Atlantic. When open, the dining room and the ocean terrace become one space. The defining architectural gesture of the restaurant renovation.
  • 03
    Polpo Interior Arched glass windows throughout the fine dining room — custom curved glass in arched frames, with chandeliers visible through the glass from outside. The interior architecture that makes the ocean view a framed experience rather than just a view.
  • 04
    Kids Club & Ice Cream Shop Floor-to-ceiling glazing for the resort's children's activity center and ice cream shop — natural light and ocean views built into spaces that could have been interior rooms.
  • 05
    Spa Additions New glazing for the expansion of the 42,000-square-foot spa. Glass for the new addition areas, maintaining the spa's connection to natural light and the Palm Beach landscape.
  • 06
    Rear Facade Entrances Updated glazing for the rear entry points of the resort — held to the same standard as the front of house, because at this level, there is no lower standard.

What It Takes to Glaze a Five-Star Resort

The Forbes Five-Star designation is awarded based on hundreds of evaluated criteria. The glass is on that list — not by name, but by implication. Surfaces have to be flawless. The property has to read as precisely maintained. The finishes can't show seams, gaps, or imperfection.

That translates directly to how glazing work gets specified and executed at this level.

Precision. Every frame has to be perfectly aligned. At a luxury resort, visitors are trained to notice quality — they notice misalignment, they notice a seal that doesn't sit flush, they notice when a pane has a distortion in it. The tolerance for error is effectively zero.

Cleanliness. The work happened inside an operating resort. Guests were checking in while the installations were underway. There were no exceptions to how the job site looked, no tolerance for debris in a corridor, no noise during dinner service. The glazing team had to operate at the same level of presentation as the resort's own staff.

Custom work. The front doors aren't a catalog item. The arched windows in Polpo aren't stock. Custom curved glass in custom arched frames, decorative interlocking patterns in the entrance doors — this requires close coordination between the fabricator, the installer, and the resort's design team. Every measurement custom, every piece fabricated to the specific opening.

Florida code. The resort sits on the Atlantic in Palm Beach County — one of the most wind-exposed coastal addresses in the state. Every piece of glass installed had to meet Florida's coastal wind load and impact requirements. The bi-fold wall system, the arched windows, the storefront — all of it engineered for the exposure category and properly permitted. Hospitality glazing in Florida isn't just a design exercise — it's a code exercise too.

What the Glass Actually Does

Glass in a luxury resort isn't decorative. It does specific work. It manages light — how much comes in, from which direction, at what hour. It manages the relationship between interior space and landscape. It makes a spa feel expansive rather than enclosed. It lets a restaurant become part of the beach rather than a room that looks at the beach.

At Polpo, the bi-fold wall doesn't just open the restaurant to the ocean. It changes what the restaurant is. The dining experience is materially different with the glass open than with it closed — the sound, the air, the connection to the terrace and the sand and the water beyond. That's not an accident. That's the design intent of the opening glass system, executed correctly.

The arched windows in the dining room do something different — they frame. They turn the ocean view into a series of composed images, each arch a curated moment of glass and water and light. The chandelier in the foreground, the Atlantic in the background, the arch containing both.

The custom front doors do something else again — they signal. From the moment a guest steps out of a car at 100 South Ocean Boulevard, the doors are announcing the register of the property they're about to enter. The curved interlocking pattern in white framing is precise and considered. It doesn't shout. It confirms.

Frequently Asked Questions

What glazing systems are used in luxury resort renovations?

Luxury resort renovations typically require a mix of custom and high-performance systems — bi-fold or opening glass walls for indoor-outdoor dining, custom decorative entrance doors, arched or curved glass frames for architectural features, floor-to-ceiling storefront for public spaces, and specialty glazing for spa areas. Each must meet Florida's coastal wind and impact requirements while satisfying the aesthetic and operational demands of a five-star property. ACG handles this full range on hospitality glazing projects across Florida.

How does a glazing contractor work inside an occupied luxury resort?

Glazing work in an occupied resort requires detailed coordination with the property's operations team. Installations are typically scheduled around service hours, phased by area to minimize disruption, and held to the same presentation standards as the resort staff. At a Forbes Five-Star property like Eau Palm Beach, there is no tolerance for job-site disorder — the contractor's team operates with the same discretion as anyone else on the property. ACG managed all seven phases around live resort operations without impacting the guest experience.

What makes luxury resort glazing different from standard commercial glazing?

Precision and finish quality. In standard commercial glazing, tolerances are measured in fractions of an inch and finish quality needs to meet code and satisfy a general contractor's punch list. In luxury resort glazing, the guest is the quality inspector — and they're paying $800 a night for the experience. Every seal has to be invisible. Every frame perfectly aligned. Every pane flawless. Custom work — arched frames, decorative entrance doors, curved glass — requires a higher level of fabrication coordination and on-site craftsmanship. And all of it has to perform to Florida's coastal code requirements. For more on commercial glazing in West Palm Beach and the Palm Beach market, see our service pages.
