ES-8000, ES-8000T and ES-9500: How to Build a Project-Specific Shortlist

Compare ESWindows storefront families against opening sizes, thermal requirements, door interfaces and current project approval documents.

By Connor Walsh, President of American Commercial Glass · Sep 12, 2026 · Florida offices: West Palm Beach, Naples, Tampa

ES-8000, ES-8000T and ES-9500 serve different configurations. Start with the scheduled opening and required performance, then confirm the applicable assembly approval before proposing an equal.

How the three storefront families differ

ES-8000

ESWindows describes ES-8000 as an impact storefront for laminated glass, with factory glazing and a door adaptor. Check the scheduled glass, opening size and impact requirements against the current assembly approval. ES-8000 manufacturer information.

ES-8000T

ES-8000T adds a thermally broken frame. The manufacturer lists compatibility with the ES-9000 door. When the specification calls for thermally broken framing, include this model in the comparison and obtain performance documentation for the actual glass and frame configuration. ES-8000T manufacturer information.

ES-9500

ES-9500 is a center-glazed impact storefront. The manufacturer lists laminated and insulating laminated options and ES-9000 door compatibility. Confirm that the selected option covers the scheduled opening. ES-9500 manufacturer information.

Decision process: what to check before you shortlist

Do not start with a brand preference. Start with the opening schedule and the structural criteria.

  1. Approval geography. Confirm whether the project is in the HVHZ and verify that the active approval covers the exact assembly and location.
  2. Required design pressure and exposure. From ASCE 7 criteria on the drawings. Compare to tested design load for the candidate system and size. Do not assume one model’s DP applies to another.
  3. Impact class. Large missile vs small missile by elevation and height. Confirm LMI/SMI against the approval for that configuration.
  4. Thermal break need. Energy code, owner performance target, or spec language for thermally broken framing. If thermal is required, prioritize ES-8000T and verify U-factor for the specified glass, not a brochure sample.
  5. Panel size and span. Width × height on the schedule vs published tested maxima. Oversized openings need engineering, not optimism.
  6. Glass makeup. Laminated vs insulating laminated, Low-E surface, thickness. Approvals are configuration-specific.
  7. Door integration. Entrance pairs and adaptors. Manufacturer pages list ES-9000 compatibility for ES-8000T and ES-9500; confirm hardware and sill details on shop drawings.
  8. Finish. Anodized, painted, wood grain as available. Match warranty and coastal exposure expectations in writing.
  9. Material-only vs installed. Who owns anchors, sealants, field glazing exceptions, and NOA submittal packaging.

How ACG uses this on bid day

We do not pick a favorite storefront and reverse-engineer the openings. Frequently asked questions

Is ES-8000 always pre-glazed?

The manufacturer product page states factory pre-assembled and pre-glazed for ES-8000, ES-8000T and ES-9500. That is a manufacturer claim for the systems as marketed. Field conditions, replacements and special sizes can change how glass is handled. Confirm on the shop drawings for your release. Do not treat “always” as absolute for every opening.

When should I shortlist ES-8000T over ES-9500?

When the spec or energy target requires a thermally broken storefront and the opening sizes and design pressures fit the ES-8000T tested envelope. If thermal is not required and the schedule needs the higher published design load and sightline profile of ES-9500, put ES-9500 on the shortlist and still verify approvals. Use the worksheet; do not default to a brand habit.

Where do I get the current NOA numbers?

From the active Miami-Dade Product Control listing or Florida Product Approval for the exact configuration, plus the manufacturer submittal. ACG coordinates that package on installed scopes. Numbers change; we do not freeze them into this article as permanent facts.
