The LEED v5 Division 08 Submittal Checklist:
Demand Evidence, Not Green-Glass Claims

An NOA is wind and impact. An EPD is a Type III declaration. Put them in different folders and stop treating a green brochure as a LEED submittal.

A Florida GC gets a Division 08 package that says “LEED glass” and an NOA. Those are not the same proof. One is for the building official. The other is for the LEED team. Mix them and the sustainability reviewer has nothing to evaluate, even if the opening would pass inspection.

Two folders: permit evidence vs sustainability evidence

Keep two folders. Label them. Do not let the glazing sub drop one PDF pile on the GC and call the LEED tracker done.

Folder A is permit. A Miami-Dade Notice of Acceptance or a Florida Product Approval proves wind and impact for the approved configuration. That is the Florida path. It is not an Environmental Product Declaration. It is not a Health Product Declaration. It is not a recycled-content certification. It is not a LEED award. The same stamp also does not satisfy AT/FP; that is a different overlay and lives on the AT/FP blast-rated glazing hub and UFC vs Florida NOA.

Folder B is sustainability. Product-specific Type III EPDs. HPDs pre-checked for LEED at the level the tracker asks for. Recycled-content paperwork only if someone claimed recycled content. Shop drawings whose product names match those cover sheets. A manufacturer letter that cites document numbers, not a slogan.

The glazing submittal package is still the shop drawings, approvals, calcs, and product data. This post does not replace that package. It tells you which extra sheets the LEED administrator can actually score.

v5 is open now; the only-version date is July 1, 2027

Confirm the registered version before you write a credit story into the bid. LEED v5 is already available. It is not a future rating system you can ignore on a 2026 start.

USGBC’s published commercial close is June 30, 2027 for new LEED v4 and v4.1 BD+C, ID+C, and O+M registrations. On July 1, 2027, LEED v5 becomes the only version for those new commercial registrations, with the limited exceptions on the LEED certification deadlines page. GBCI recorded the one-year extension of the v4 / v4.1 close to June 30, 2027; that is the current public date, not a June 2026 leftover. See the GBCI extension note and the LEED v5 support article.

Do not publish, spec, or bid a July 1, 2026 LEED cutover. That date is wrong. If a consultant still has it on a cover sheet, make them correct the edition before you collect documents against the wrong version.

MRc4 does not name glass or aluminum

LEED v5 BD+C, July 2026 edition, credit MRc4 Building Product Selection and Procurement is 1 to 5 points. Read the credit in the July 2026 BD+C rating system. The product categories listed there are paints and coatings, adhesives and sealants, flooring, walls, ceilings, insulation, furniture, composite wood, and plumbing fixtures.

Glass and aluminum are not named in that list. Do not promise a GC, an owner, or a lender that the exterior glazing package earns MRc4 points. Ask the LEED administrator how Division 08 maps on this project. If they want the storefront in a different credit, write that mapping on the submittal cover. If they do not, stop selling “five LEED points in the curtain wall.”

Glass and aluminum extrusions do appear in MRp2 Quantify and Assess Embodied Carbon as enclosure materials to inventory. That is a required quantification step for structure, enclosure, and hardscape. It is not an MRc4 award. Whether those same EPDs later feed MRc2 Reduce Embodied Carbon is a LEED-admin question. Collect the declarations. Do not score the points yourself.

Climate health: demand a Type III EPD, then the comparison

Building Product Selection and Procurement scores products across criteria areas. Climate health is the EPD path. The thresholds below are from BPSP Criteria Areas and Levels v1.3.

Climate health, BPSP v1.3. Product-specific Type III EPD first. Comparisons second. Not a permit stamp.

Level What to collect
L1 Product-specific Type III EPD. ISO 14025 plus EN 15804 or ISO 21930.
L2 Comparative assessment. At least 20% GWP reduction from the stated baseline.
L3 At least 40% GWP reduction plus at least 10% in three other impact categories.

Level 1 is disclosure. Demand the declaration, the program-operator number, the validity dates, and the product names on the cover. Level 2 and Level 3 are comparisons. They need a baseline document (a product-specific Type III EPD, or an industry-average Type III EPD where the manufacturer is a named participant) and an optimization document that is itself a product-specific Type III EPD. A sales slide that says “20 percent better” is not the comparative assessment.

Human health: HPD pre-checked for LEED, then verified

Human health is the ingredient path. Again, BPSP v1.3:

Human health, BPSP v1.3. Pre-checked HPD first. Third-party verified inventory second.

Level What to collect
L1 HPD pre-checked for LEED. Content inventory to 0.1% (1,000 ppm).
L2 Third-party verified inventory to 0.01% (100 ppm).

Ask for the HPD that names the product on the shop drawing. “Pre-checked for LEED” is a status on the document, not a verbal. Level 2 is a verification step, not a darker shade of the same PDF. If the tracker only asked for L1, do not let the sub stall the buyout hunting an L2 they were never scoped to provide. If the tracker asked for L2, do not accept L1 and hope the reviewer rounds up.

A flat-glass EPD is not a finished IGU

Cite public declarations when you ask for paper. Do not invent numbers off them, and do not treat any one of them as the completed storefront.

  • Vitro SCS-EPD-08776 is a Type III flat-glass EPD. It is float, not the fabricated IGU.
  • Pilkington EPD-IES-0021621 is a Type III flat-glass EPD for uncoated annealed products from Pilkington North America. Same limit: it is not the finished unit.
  • Aluminum framing is a different product. The AEC industry-average extrusion EPDs are industry-average Type III declarations for North American extrusions, including a thermally improved set. Industry-average is not a product-specific optimization claim.

The Guardian ExtraClear EU EPD is a European declaration for ExtraClear uncoated flat glass produced in the EU and the United Kingdom. Do not drop it into a Florida submittal as United States evidence without an applicability note from the manufacturer that ties it to the plant actually shipping the job. If the lite is a US melt, ask for the US document.

If the opening is an insulating glass unit, demand the declaration that covers that unit, or a clear stack: float EPD plus processed-glass or IGU EPD plus extrusion EPD, each matched to a line on the glass schedule. A coating, a laminate, a spacer, or an argon fill is not hidden inside a float-only cover sheet.

Recycled-content claims need paper

If the spec, the LEED tracker, or the bid letter claims recycled content in the glass or the aluminum, collect the manufacturer document that states the content basis and the product name. A website sentence is not the document. Do not invent a percentage to fill a cell. If nobody claimed recycled content, do not add a number so the package looks greener.

Print the field card: documents to demand

  • Registered LEED version and date. v4, v4.1, or v5. No July 1, 2026 cutover on the cover.
  • Credit-mapping note from the LEED administrator. Which credits Division 08 is asked to support. Do not assume MRc4.
  • Product-specific Type III EPD for the glass being bought. ISO 14025 plus EN 15804 or ISO 21930. Program-operator number and validity dates on the cover.
  • Scope match. Flat glass, processed glass, or IGU. A float EPD does not close a finished unit.
  • Aluminum extrusion EPD if the LEED team asked for enclosure inventory: product-specific, or the AEC industry-average set with no optimization claim attached.
  • Comparative packet only if L2 or L3 was requested: baseline EPD, optimization EPD, and the stated GWP reduction. No brochure math.
  • HPD pre-checked for LEED at L1 (0.1% / 1,000 ppm) or third-party verified at L2 (0.01% / 100 ppm), matching the tracker.
  • Recycled-content document only if recycled content was claimed. Product name on the sheet.
  • Shop drawings and glass schedule whose product names match the EPD and HPD covers.
  • NOA or Florida Product Approval in Folder A. Wind and impact for the approved configuration. Not an EPD. Not an HPD. Not a LEED award.
  • Manufacturer letter that cites document numbers and plants. Reject “LEED-compliant glass.”

Stamp occupancy, Florida wind path, and any federal overlay on the Bid-Day Glazing Checker. An NOA still does not satisfy AT/FP; see the AT/FP hub.

Bid-day mistakes

  • Treating an NOA as an EPD. Wind and impact approval is permit evidence. It does not disclose GWP and it does not award LEED.
  • Treating a flat-glass EPD as the IGU. Coat, laminate, spacer, and gas are not in a float-only declaration.
  • Using a European ExtraClear EPD on a US lite with no applicability note.
  • Promising MRc4 points for the curtain wall. Glass and aluminum are not named in the July 2026 MRc4 category list. Ask the LEED admin.
  • Writing July 1, 2026 as the v5-only date. The published commercial close is June 30, 2027. v5-only new registrations begin July 1, 2027.
  • Accepting a marketing one-pager as Type III. Level 1 needs the third-party declaration.
  • Calling an industry-average extrusion EPD a product-specific optimization. AEC averages are baselines, not L2 or L3 by themselves.
  • Filling recycled-content cells with a guessed percentage.
  • Letting product names drift between the shop drawing, the EPD cover, and the HPD cover.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does an NOA or Florida Product Approval count as LEED sustainability evidence?

No. A Miami-Dade NOA or Florida Product Approval proves wind and impact for the approved configuration. It is not an EPD, not an HPD, not a recycled-content certification, and not a LEED award.

Does an exterior glazing package earn MRc4 points?

Do not assume that. LEED v5 BD+C July 2026 MRc4 Building Product Selection and Procurement is 1 to 5 points. The named product categories are paints and coatings, adhesives and sealants, flooring, walls, ceilings, insulation, furniture, composite wood, and plumbing fixtures. Glass and aluminum are not named. Ask the LEED administrator how Division 08 maps on your project.

When does LEED v5 become the only commercial registration path?

LEED v5 is already available. LEED v4 and v4.1 commercial registration for BD+C, ID+C, and O+M closes June 30, 2027. On July 1, 2027, v5 becomes the only version for new commercial registrations in those systems, with limited exceptions on the USGBC deadlines page. There is no July 1, 2026 cutover.

What EPD does Climate Health Level 1 require?

A product-specific Type III EPD that conforms to ISO 14025 and either EN 15804 or ISO 21930. Level 2 is a comparative assessment showing at least 20 percent GWP reduction. Level 3 is at least 40 percent GWP reduction plus at least 10 percent in three other impact categories, per BPSP v1.3.

What HPD does Human Health Level 1 require?

An HPD pre-checked for LEED with a content inventory to 0.1 percent, which is 1,000 ppm. Level 2 requires a third-party verified inventory to 0.01 percent, which is 100 ppm.

Is a flat-glass EPD enough for a finished insulating glass unit?

No. A flat-glass EPD covers unprocessed float, not the completed IGU. Coatings, lites, spacer, gas fill, and laminate are outside that declaration unless a processed-glass or IGU EPD says they are in scope. Match the document to the product on the shop drawing.

Can I use the Guardian ExtraClear EU EPD on a Florida job?

Not without an applicability note. That ExtraClear EPD is a European declaration. It does not, by itself, describe a United States melt. Ask for the EPD that covers the plant actually supplying the job.
