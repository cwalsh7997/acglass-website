Shop Approval Is Not a Ship Date:
What Starts the Manufacturing Clock?

Approved shops, locked dimensions, finish, deposit, and factory acknowledgment are separate gates. Confusing them for a ship date burns schedule float.

Quick Answer

Shop drawing approval is not a ship date. Manufacturing often starts only after separate release gates clear: shop approval, locked dimensions, finish selection, deposit or credit clearance, and factory acknowledgment. Track each gate. Do not invent lead-time days here. Confirm current fabrication weeks with your supplier and see ACG's lead-times guide for system context.

Approval stamp vs ship date

Architect or GC approval of shop drawings means the drawings are accepted for fabrication intent. It does not mean aluminum is cutting, glass is ordered, or a truck is booked.

A ship date is when finished material leaves the plant. Between approval and ship sit release gates and fabrication. Scheduling install from the approval stamp alone is how storefront arrives after drywall.

Ask your supplier one question in writing: Which document and payment state start your manufacturing clock on this order? The answer is supplier-specific.

Flow (typical): Shop approval → dimensions locked → finish named → deposit clear → factory ack → fabrication → ship. Some suppliers allow parallel gates. Confirm your PO.

Gate 1: Shop approval

Approved-as-noted with open hold items that affect size, glass makeup, or hardware is not a clean release. Resolve holds that change fabrication before you treat the stamp as final.

Revise-and-resubmit cycles restart review. They do not start manufacturing. Track each return date on the same sheet as the gates above.

Gate 2: Dimensions locked

Many packages need rough opening confirmation from structural or field measure before glass is cut. “Per plans” is risky when the field opening drifted after steel.

Write the dimension source into the release: structural drawing revision, field ticket date, or architect directive. If dimensions change after factory ack, expect a stop or remake.

Gate 3: Finish selection

Custom finishes often gate extrusion processing. A color TBD hold can leave approved shops sitting while the plant waits for a finish code.

Name the finish system and color code in the same package as the shops. Do not assume standard mill finish when the elevations show a custom color.

Gate 4: Deposit / credit

Some manufacturers will not enter fabrication until deposit clears or credit is approved. That is a commercial gate, not a drawing gate.

AP delay after “approved shops” is a common hidden schedule killer. Put deposit timing on the same tracker as drawing approval.

Gate 5: Factory acknowledgment

Factory acknowledgment is the plant saying the release package is complete enough to manufacture. Until that ack exists, you have paperwork, not a manufacturing start.

Ask for the ack in writing with a reference number. That document is what you schedule against, not the architect stamp alone.

Ship date comes later, after fabrication and freight booking. For ranges by system type, use the lead times article and reconfirm at buyout. This page intentionally does not invent days or weeks.

How to ask without guessing weeks

Send elevations and the door and window schedule. Ask for: (1) which gates start the clock on your order, (2) current fabrication window after factory ack, and (3) whether finish or deposit currently holds release.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does shop drawing approval start the manufacturing clock?

Often no. Many factories wait for approved shops plus locked dimensions, finish selection, deposit clearance, and a factory acknowledgment of release. Treat each as a separate gate until your PO says otherwise.

What is the difference between shop approval and a ship date?

Shop approval means drawings are accepted for fabrication intent. A ship date is when finished material leaves the plant. Between those points sit release gates and fabrication itself. Do not schedule truck arrival from the approval stamp alone.

Which release gates should a GC track?

Track shop approval, field or structural dimension lock, finish selection, deposit or credit clearance, and factory acknowledgment of the release package. Ask the supplier which gate starts their clock on your order.
