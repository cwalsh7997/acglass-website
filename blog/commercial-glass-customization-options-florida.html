How Commercial Glass Can Be
Customized for Your Business

Frost, frit, color, curvature, and graphic — the six custom glass techniques, what they cost, and how long they add to your project schedule.

Connor Walsh, ACG · 2026-04-22 · 8 min read
Quick Answer

Commercial glass customization options: tint (bronze, grey, blue, green), low-E coatings (solar heat gain control 0.20–0.40 SHGC), frit/ceramic patterns (privacy + solar shading), digital printing (full-color graphics), spandrel back-coating (any opaque color), acid-etched/sandblasted (privacy), laminated colors (PVB tints between panes), and switchable smart glass (electrochromic / SPD / PDLC).

Commercial glass does not have to be clear and rectangular. Modern fabrication techniques allow commercial glass to carry brand graphics, privacy patterns, custom colors, curved geometry, and functional solar control as part of the glass itself rather than as overlays or applied films. For Florida commercial projects, understanding what is possible — and what each technique adds to cost and schedule — is the difference between designing with custom glass and designing around it. This article walks through the six most-specified custom glass techniques on Florida commercial work, with real cost premiums, lead times, and use-case examples from recent projects.

Six Ways to Customize Commercial Glass

Customization in architectural glass fabrication has expanded significantly over the past decade. What used to require specialty studios can now be produced at scale by major glass fabricators — at prices that make custom glass viable even for mid-sized commercial projects. The six customization techniques below are the ones ACG specifies most often on Florida commercial work. Each carries different cost premiums, lead times, and design possibilities.

1. Acid-Etched and Sandblasted Frost

Sandblasted or acid-etched frost is the baseline custom glass technique: a translucent diffused surface produced by mechanically or chemically roughening the glass. Sandblast is more aggressive and produces a slightly more textured look; acid etch is smoother and more uniform.

Applications: conference room privacy panels, office partition walls, shower walls, brand element glass, storefront vestibule treatments. Frost can be full-coverage, patterned (geometric or organic shapes), or graded (gradient from clear to fully frosted). Common graded applications run 24–42 inches of gradient to balance privacy at seated height with natural light above.

Lead time: 1–3 weeks added to base fabrication.

2. Digital Ceramic Frit (DCF) Printing

Digital ceramic frit printing is the technology that has most expanded the possibilities for custom commercial glass. A large-format inkjet printer deposits ceramic ink directly onto tempered glass, which is then fired into the glass surface during tempering. The result is a permanent full-color image fused into the glass, durable for decades, with no fade or peel.

Applications: graphic storefronts, wayfinding glass, brand walls, restaurant interior glass, logo glazing, architectural feature walls. The resolution is roughly equivalent to a medium-quality photo print at full building scale. Full-color, gradients, photo imagery, and custom artwork all work.

Lead time: 4–8 weeks.

3. Interlayer Color (PVB Colored Film)

Colored PVB or SentryGlas interlayers produce laminated glass with a permanent color cast. The color is sealed between two glass plies, so it cannot scratch, peel, or fade from UV exposure. Colored interlayers work particularly well for brand element glass and architectural statement walls where the color needs to be consistent across large runs.

Color options range from subtle tints (smoke, bronze, blue-green) to bold statement colors (red, orange, deep blue). For impact-rated applications, colored PVB can still meet TAS 201/202/203 when paired with appropriate glass plies.

Lead time: 3–5 weeks.

4. Gradient or Dot Pattern Frit (Functional + Aesthetic)

Ceramic frit applied as a dot or line pattern serves a dual function: sun control (reducing solar heat gain) and aesthetic (providing visual pattern). White, gray, black, or custom-color frits in dot or line patterns can reduce SHGC values by 20–40% while producing an architectural element that reads intentional.

Common applications: spandrel glass, curtainwall feature walls, storefront where full-height transparency is not desired, exterior privacy at pedestrian level. On energy-sensitive projects, frit patterns can be a meaningful SHGC tool without resorting to heavier tinting.

Lead time: 3–6 weeks.

5. Curved and Bent Glass

Curved glass panels — bent during the tempering process to a specified radius — enable rounded storefront corners, curved facade elements, and feature entry walls that could not be produced with flat glass and mitered seams. Impact-rated curved glass is achievable in Florida but requires engineered NOA documentation.

Tighter radii (under 6 feet) are more expensive and have stricter thickness limits. Larger sweeping curves (15+ foot radius) are relatively routine and moderately priced. Common applications: hotel lobby corners, luxury retail entries, restaurant facades, airport concourses.

Lead time: 8–16 weeks.

6. Back-Painted Color Panels (Spandrel and Feature Walls)

Back-painted glass is tempered glass coated on the back side with a durable painted finish, producing a solid opaque color panel with a mirror-smooth glass front face. Common applications include elevator lobby feature walls, restaurant kitchen-facing glass, interior accent walls, and spandrel glass in curtainwall assemblies where the opaque panel reads as part of the glazing module.

Color options are essentially unlimited — custom RAL or Pantone matching is standard. Paint is cured to full hardness and fused to the glass, not peel-on-peel-off vinyl.

Lead time: 3–6 weeks.

Real Project Examples

Office Logo Walls

See our glass partition walls guide for specification details on interior partition systems that frequently carry custom glass.

Corporate lobbies frequently use acid-etched or digital ceramic frit to integrate company branding into the glass partition system. A well-executed logo wall reads as architectural rather than signage — the brand is embedded in the building, not mounted to it.

Conference Room Privacy

Gradient frost on conference room glass — clear at top, frosted at seated height, clear at bottom — has become standard in Class A commercial interiors. The pattern delivers privacy at meeting level while preserving natural light penetration and the open feel of full-glass partitions.

Storefront Branding

Retail and hospitality brands use digital ceramic frit for permanent brand integration into storefront glazing. Unlike vinyl signage, which has a 3–5 year UV life in Florida before fade and peel, DCF is permanent. On projects like Wave Food Hall in Cocoa Beach, branded glass panels carry the restaurant identity as part of the architecture.

Restaurant Identity

Restaurants use combinations of back-painted accent panels, colored interlayers, and custom frit to establish interior identity. High-end restaurants particularly benefit from colored interlayer work because the color reads consistent throughout changing daylight conditions.

Lead Time and Budget Impact

Custom glass work adds meaningful time to the glazing schedule. A standard impact storefront package might ship 6–8 weeks from order. Adding digital ceramic frit or curved glass panels can extend the critical path to 12–20 weeks. For projects with tight schedules, the glass customization timeline must be coordinated early in design to avoid pushing glazing off the construction schedule.

The Pre-Glazed Advantage for Custom Glass

Custom-finish glass is more vulnerable to field damage during install than standard clear glass. Digital ceramic frit and back-painted surfaces can scratch. Acid-etched faces can be smudged or marked. Pre-glazed factory assembly minimizes the number of times the custom face is handled. The glass is set into the frame once, in factory conditions, with the finished face protected. The unit arrives on site ready to install — no field glazing handling of the sensitive face required.

This is a real differentiator on graphic storefront work. Stick-built installs mean custom glass has to be handled multiple times: unpacked, set against the frame, re-positioned, sealed. Each touch is a chance for scratch or smudge. Pre-glazed arrives inside a complete unit with the custom face already protected.

Specifying Custom Glass on a Commercial Project

The most common mistake on custom glass specifications is designing for the technique rather than for the design intent. Before locking a technique, it is worth having the glazing sub review design intent against the available options — fabricators have strong opinions about which technique produces what effect reliably.
