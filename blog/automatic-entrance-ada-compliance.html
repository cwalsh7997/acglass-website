Automatic Entrance Systems:
ADA Compliance and Beyond

Understanding ADA requirements, ANSI standards, sensor technology, integration with fire and security systems, and maintenance best practices.

Why Automatic Entrances Matter

Automatic doors serve multiple functions: they provide accessible entry for people with mobility challenges, improve traffic flow for high-volume entrances, enhance building aesthetics, and support building security and climate control systems. Understanding ADA requirements, ANSI standards, and installation best practices ensures systems perform reliably and provide the intended accessibility and functionality.

ADA Requirements for Automatic Door Operators

The Americans with Disabilities Act requires accessible means of entry. For high-volume entrances and public buildings, automatic door operators are often the primary accessibility solution. ADA Accessibility Guidelines (ADAAG) Section 206.3 specifies requirements for automatic and power-assisted doors.

Key ADA Requirements:

  • Opening force: Less than 5 pounds of force to open the door
  • Activation: Sensor must activate door to open when person approaches from either side
  • Door width: Minimum clear opening width of 32 inches (measured from face of door in open position)
  • Clear floor space: Minimum 60-inch clear floor space on approach side for wheelchair user to position themselves
  • Door opening and closing: Door must remain open long enough for people to pass through safely (typically 3-6 seconds minimum)
  • Manual operation: If sensors fail, manual push plates or levers must be available to open door manually

ANSI Standards Governing Automatic Doors

ANSI/BHMA A156.10: Power Operated Door Operators. This is the governing standard for automatic door operators. It specifies performance requirements, testing procedures, safety features, and installation details. All automatic operators should be listed to ANSI A156.10.

ANSI/BHMA A156.19: Low-Energy Power Assisted Doors. This standard applies to doors that are manually pushed but assisted by a power operator (not fully automatic). Requirements are less stringent than fully automatic systems.

Sensor Types and Selection

Motion Sensors (Microwave/Radar): Detect motion in a coverage area and trigger door opening. Most common type for entrances. Advantages: Reliable, unobstructed field of view, works with people of all mobility levels. Disadvantages: Can be triggered by nearby movement outside intended area (false opens).

Infrared Sensors: Detect heat from people approaching. More directional than motion sensors, less prone to false triggers. Advantages: Good for tight spaces or to prevent false activation from adjacent traffic. Disadvantages: Can fail in extreme cold or heat; less reliable than motion sensors in some conditions.

Pressure Mats: Detect weight on floor surface in front of door. Advantages: Manual activation; prevents accidental opening. Disadvantages: Can wear out, require maintenance, present tripping hazard. Increasingly less common.

Push-button activation: Manual button pressed by person to trigger door. Advantages: Reliable, no false activation. Disadvantages: Doesn't meet full ADA spirit for people with severe mobility challenges. Often used as backup or supplementary activation method.

Fire and Security Integration

Fire Code Compliance: In case of fire alarm activation, automatic doors must fail safe to ensure occupants can exit. Doors should automatically release and remain unlocked during fire alarm. ACG coordinates with fire alarm contractors to ensure proper integration. Exit doors must open in the direction of egress and should not require activation—they must open freely.

Security Integration: Many entrances require automatic doors that are integrated with security systems—only opening for authorized personnel. This requires electronic lock integration and may involve card readers or mobile credentials. Integration must not compromise emergency egress (doors must unlock and open during fire alarm evacuation).

Video Surveillance: Automatic entrances often have associated video surveillance. Sensor activation can trigger recording; video provides documentation of entry patterns. Proper system design ensures surveillance and automatic door control work together reliably.

Installation and Commissioning

Automatic door installation requires precise framing, proper operator mounting, and careful sensor positioning. The frame must be square and level for smooth door operation. Operators must be mounted according to manufacturer specifications. Sensors must have correct coverage area, must activate reliably, and must not create false openings.

Commissioning involves testing door opening force, timing, sensor activation, manual override function, and integration with other building systems. ACG performs comprehensive commissioning to ensure systems operate as designed and meet ADA requirements.

Common Issues and Maintenance

False Activation: Sensors trigger door opening when not intended. Solution: Adjust sensor sensitivity and coverage area; reposition sensor away from adjacent foot traffic or moving objects.

Slow Opening or Stalling: Door opens slowly or stops mid-opening. Solution: Check operator motor, lubricate door hardware, verify power supply to operator.

Sensor Failure: Sensor doesn't activate door or activates unpredictably. Solution: Clean sensor lens (dirt/dust blocks activation); verify sensor power and wiring; replace sensor if damaged.

Door Not Closing Properly: Door closes too slowly or doesn't close completely. Solution: Adjust closer tension; check for obstacles; verify door alignment.

Manual Override Failure: Push plates or manual release don't work. Solution: Verify manual release is mechanically connected to door opening mechanism; test function regularly; repair or replace broken hardware.

Maintenance Best Practices

Automatic doors require regular maintenance to function reliably. Establish a maintenance schedule: Sensor cleaning quarterly, operator lubrication annually, manual override testing monthly, complete system inspection annually.

Create a maintenance log documenting all service and repairs. This helps identify recurring problems and can provide documentation of proper maintenance if issues occur later. Many building owners contract with door service companies for ongoing maintenance and repair.

ADA Compliance Verification

Before final acceptance, verify automatic doors meet ADA requirements: Test opening force (should be less than 5 pounds); measure clear opening width (minimum 32 inches); verify sensor activation from both sides; confirm door remains open for adequate time (minimum 3 seconds); test manual override function; verify floor space meets clearance requirements.

Building officials may inspect automatic entrances to verify ADA compliance. Documentation of commissioning tests and performance verification is valuable evidence of compliance.
