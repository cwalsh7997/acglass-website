What Is a Storefront

What Is Included in a Storefront Glazing System?

A storefront glazing system combines aluminum framing, glass and entrance components to enclose a commercial opening. The frame attaches to the building, while glazing seals and drainage details manage the joints around the glass. A storefront is an enclosure system. It does not replace the building's load-bearing structure.

For system options and an installation scope, see commercial storefront systems and installation. For a comparison with individual window units, read storefront systems vs. commercial windows.

The parts to identify on the drawings

  • Framing: vertical mullions and horizontal members, with the manufacturer's specified connections and anchors.
  • Glass: the specified glass type and makeup, matched to the frame and project performance requirements.
  • Glazing seals and drainage: gaskets, sealants, sill components and water-management details for the selected system.
  • Entrances: door leaves, frames and hardware coordinated with the fixed framing and opening schedule.
  • Perimeter interfaces: the head, jamb and sill connections to the adjacent wall, waterproofing and supporting construction.

Where Is Commercial Storefront Used?

Storefront systems are commonly used at ground-floor and low-rise commercial openings, including retail fronts, office entrances, restaurants and medical buildings. The name describes the framing system, rather than limiting it to shops. The selected manufacturer's performance and installation requirements determine whether a system fits a particular opening.

How Is Storefront Different from Commercial Windows?

A commercial window is an individual framed unit installed in an opening. Storefront framing can connect multiple fixed lites and entrance doors across a larger opening. They can look similar from the street, but the frame connections, drainage, anchorage and approval documents may differ. Match the system named in the plans before pricing a substitution.

How Is Storefront Different from Curtainwall?

Storefront and curtainwall are both non-load-bearing enclosure systems. They still must support their own components and transfer the applicable loads through their connections. Curtainwall can be designed to span multiple floors, with different anchorage, movement and drainage details. Height alone does not determine which system to use.

Compare the specified assembly and its tested performance with the design requirements. Our storefront, curtainwall and window-wall comparison explains the planning differences.

Which Glass and Frame Options Matter?

Check the specified glass makeup, frame finish, thermal requirements, impact criteria and entrance configuration together. Thermal breaks reduce heat transfer through framing, but they are a product feature to evaluate against the project criteria. They are not a substitute for reviewing the performance of the complete assembly.

As a manufacturer example, YKK AP's YES 45 FT is a thermally broken storefront with monolithic and insulating-glass options. That example does not establish suitability for another product or a specific Florida project.

What Should the GC Send for a Storefront Bid?

  1. Current plans, storefront elevations, schedules, specifications and addenda.
  2. Opening dimensions, glass requirements, frame finish and specified system.
  3. Door and hardware sets, including operator and access-control interfaces.
  4. Head, jamb and sill details, substrates and adjacent waterproofing.
  5. Bid deadline, target installation dates and access or staging restrictions.

Confirm the permitting authority, applicable product documents and each trade's responsibilities with the project team. Use the Florida Product Approval guide and glazing submittal checklist to organize the review.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is a storefront glazing system?

A storefront glazing system combines aluminum framing, glass and entrance components to enclose a commercial opening. It attaches to the building and is commonly used at ground-floor and low-rise commercial openings.

What is the difference between storefront and curtainwall?

Both are non-load-bearing enclosure systems. Their anchorage, movement, drainage and performance details differ. Curtainwall can span multiple floors. The project requirements and the selected system's tested performance determine which assembly is appropriate.

What should I send for a storefront installation bid?

Send current drawings, storefront elevations, door and window schedules, specifications, addenda, project location and bid deadline. Include the installation period, opening details and any site access restrictions.
