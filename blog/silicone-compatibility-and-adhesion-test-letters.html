Silicone Compatibility and Adhesion:
What the Test Letters Actually Cover

Read sealant test letters correctly: compatibility vs adhesion vs substrate. Generic letter anatomy, not a forged certificate.

Quick Answer

A sealant letter is only as useful as the question it answered. Compatibility, adhesion, and substrate approval are different claims. Read the product names, substrates, joint design limits, and date on the letter before you treat it as permission to glaze. This page uses a generic letter anatomy. It is not a manufacturer certificate.

Three different claims

  • Compatibility: materials in contact do not chemically attack each other under the tested conditions
  • Adhesion: the sealant bonds adequately to the named substrate under the tested conditions
  • Substrate / finish approval: the specific finish, coating, or glass edge condition was included in the test or approval language

A letter that only addresses compatibility is not automatically an adhesion approval for every finish on the elevation.

Submittal checklist

  • Letter attached for each sealant-to-substrate pair that the detail requires
  • Product names match the glazing and sealant submittals exactly
  • Finish and glass coating conditions covered or separately tested
  • Structural silicone applications (if any) follow the structural silicone design path required by the project

What letters do not cover

Letters do not replace joint design, workmanship, or water-test acceptance. They do not approve a different sealant brand by implication. They do not waive product-approval requirements for the fenestration system.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the difference between silicone compatibility and adhesion letters?

Compatibility addresses chemical interaction between materials. Adhesion addresses bond to a named substrate under tested conditions. A letter may cover one, both, or neither for your exact finish.

Can I reuse a letter from a previous job?

Only if the exact products, substrates, and conditions match and the letter is still accepted by the manufacturer and the design team. Re-verify dates and product names.

Does a compatibility letter approve structural silicone design?

Not by itself. Structural silicone applications follow the project structural-glazing requirements and manufacturer structural guidelines.

What should a GC attach for sealant review?

Details, glass and finish IDs, proposed sealant product data, and any existing manufacturer letters for those exact combinations.
