When Florida GCs choose ESWindows curtainwall

On bid day, naming curtainwall is not a brand preference. It is a field decision about span, dead load, slab-edge anchorage, mockup cycles, and who owns install risk.

Florida GCs keep ESWindows curtainwall on the schedule when these job conditions line up:

  • Mid-rise and high-rise stacked elevations. Floor-to-floor curtainwall or window-wall planes, not a one-story retail front.
  • Unitized or stick curtainwall procurement. Longer engineering, anchorage, and mockup cycles than a pre-glazed storefront package.
  • Impact practice on coastal and HVHZ work. Large and small missile language appears on manufacturer curtainwall pages, with an explicit note that certifications might not cover every configuration.
  • One installer who also owns Florida submittals. ACG furnishes and installs ESWindows commercial systems and packages the approval docs for the AHJ path named on the drawings.

Those are construction reasons. If the drawings say storefront and the wind criteria fit a tested storefront assembly, do not stretch curtainwall into the void. Sister Division 08 install context lives on our curtainwall installation page when you need process language beyond this brand spoke.

Curtainwall vs storefront: hard decision boundary

Specifying curtainwall when the building wants storefront (or the reverse) is how you get RFIs and a late substitution. Use a hard boundary.

Field decision aid. Confirm against structural criteria and the envelope section on the drawings.

Signal Lean storefront Lean curtainwall / window wall
Building height / floors Ground floor, one- and two-story commercial fronts Mid-rise and high-rise stacked floors, slab-edge spans
Span and dead load Typical storefront mullion spans within tested storefront envelopes Floor-to-floor spans, heavier glass, unitized or stick CW
Entrance integration Integral door adaptor into storefront when specified Entrances often engineered as separate assemblies in the CW plane
Procurement shape Factory pre-glazed storefront packages; faster shop cycle on locked openings Longer engineering, anchorage, and mockup cycles

If the drawings say storefront, start with the ES-8000 GC case on the ES-8000 flagship. If the drawings say curtainwall, stay on this page. System choice follows the envelope.

Furnish-and-install path for GCs

Most curtainwall friction on Florida jobs is paperwork, embed coordination, and dimension control, not extrusion quality alone.

  • Shop drawings vs product data. Product sheets prove the system exists. Shops prove your modules and anchors. Release fabrication on approved shops, not on a brochure PDF.
  • Field conditions before release. Unitized packages punish wrong slab edges and missing embeds. Who owns field verify must be explicit in the subcontract.
  • Mockups when the spec requires them. Visual and performance mockups burn schedule if they start after glass is already cut.
  • Finish and coastal exposure. Anodized, painted, and wood grain are listed by the manufacturer for GW-7000. Put the finish and warranty expectation in writing before color samples get lost in email.
  • Material-only vs furnish-and-install. ACG can quote both. Furnish-and-install puts buyout, submittals, receiving, install, and punch under one glazing sub. Say which mode you want on the same schedule.

ACG’s value on an ESWindows curtainwall buy is not a slogan. It is one Florida licensed contractor running manufacturer coordination and field install so the GC is not bridging a distributor quote and a separate crew.
