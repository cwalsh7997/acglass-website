This page is about purchasing arrangement and risk ownership. It is not a line-by-line install deliverable list. For the storefront install scope checklist, use what’s included in storefront glass installation.

Two buying modes, one package of parts

Commercial storefront and window packages usually include aluminum framing, glass, anchors or anchor hardware as specified, gaskets, and related accessories. The question is who buys that package and who owns the work after the truck leaves the plant.

Supply-only (material-only) is a purchase of product. The seller’s obligation ends at the delivery point named in the purchase order, unless the PO adds receiving help or staging. Field layout, anchors, sealants, hardware set, and punch sit with the GC or a separate installer.

Furnish-and-install is a subcontract. The glazing contractor buys the system, runs submittals, coordinates fabrication release, receives material, installs, and closes punch. Manufacturer product warranties still pass through. The install warranty rides with the installing contractor.

Neither mode changes Florida product approval or permit requirements by itself. Which party holds AHJ responsibility for stamped calcs and NOA/FBC PA submission is project-specific and must follow the contract and local practice. What changes is who is on the hook when a dimension is wrong, a crate is damaged, or a sealant joint fails punch.

Design coordination and buyout

Design intent stays with the architect and owner on both modes. What moves is shop drawing ownership and who pays for revision cycles after field measure.

On supply-only, the GC often buys from a supplier while a separate crew installs. That split works when openings are locked, the schedule is clear, and someone owns field verification before fabrication release. It fails when the PO says “per plans” and the field opening does not match the schedule.

On furnish-and-install, the glazing sub prices both material and labor in one package. Buyout is cleaner for the GC. The glazing sub carries the coordination between shops, glass makeup, and site conditions.

Approvals and fabrication release

Product data, Florida Product Approval or Miami-Dade NOA documents where required, and shop drawings are paperwork. Who gathers them and who submits them are different jobs.

ACG, the GC, or the supplier is not always the party of record for sealed structural calcs or AHJ filings. That assignment is project-specific.

Fabrication should not start on a verbal. Release needs approved shops (or a written release-at-risk from the buyer named on the PO). On supply-only, that buyer is often the GC or owner. On furnish-and-install, it is the glazing sub. Wrong release owner is how you get glass cut to an obsolete schedule.

Shipping, field install, and punch

Freight terms decide when title and risk of loss transfer. Read the PO Incoterms and the delivery address. A dock drop is not the same as set-in-place.

Supply-only receiving is a GC problem: crane, forklift, dry storage, and a damage log before the truck leaves. Furnish-and-install still needs GC site access, but the glazing sub owns the receiving checklist for its material.

Punch splits the same way. Material defects (wrong lite, finish failure under manufacturer terms) route to the supplier path. Workmanship (sealant, plumb, hardware adjustment) routes to whoever installed. Mixing those paths late is expensive.

Warranty split and risk transfer

Do not treat “warranty” as one word. Product warranties come from manufacturers and pass through. Install warranties come from the installing contractor. Specific durations, transfer rules, and exclusions are manufacturer- and subcontract-specific.

Risk transfer moments that matter on every buyout:

  • Approved shop drawing date vs verbal field dimensions
  • Fabrication release signature
  • Freight handoff and signed receiving ticket
  • Substantial completion / punch acceptance for the glazing scope

Write those four dates into the buyout notes. They decide who pays when something is late or damaged.

How to ask for the right quote

Send elevations and the door and window schedule. Add one sentence: material-only (supply-only) or furnish-and-install. If you need both options, ask for two scopes on the same opening list, not one ambiguous price.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is supply-only commercial glazing?

Supply-only means the seller furnishes framed units, glass, and related materials to the jobsite or staging point. Field install, sealants, and punch remain with the GC or a separate installer unless the purchase order says otherwise.

What is furnish-and-install glazing?

Furnish-and-install puts buyout, submittals, fabrication release, receiving, field install, and punch under one glazing subcontractor. Manufacturer warranties still pass through. The install warranty rides with the installing contractor.

Who owns product approvals on a supply-only buy?

The supplier typically provides product data and approval documents. Who stamps, submits, and tracks AHJ comments is a contract assignment. Confirm it in the PO and Division 01 before buyout.

How should a GC request a material-only vs installed quote?

Send elevations, the door and window schedule, and a one-line scope note: material-only or furnish-and-install. That single line prevents a mixed bid and a late risk transfer.
