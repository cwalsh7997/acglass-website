Two systems, one restaurant problem

Folding (bi-fold) glass walls connect panels with hinges. Panels fold and stack accordion-style at one jamb, both jambs, or a designated parking bay. When open, the wall can leave a continuous clear opening across the elevation, subject to the approved configuration.

Multi-slide glass walls run panels on parallel tracks. Panels slide sideways and stack in overlapping tracks or disappear into a pocket. When closed, sightlines are often cleaner because panels sit in plane rather than hinged. When open, the stack or pocket location becomes the planning problem.

Layout and opening configuration

Start with the elevation and the furniture plan together. An opening that looks correct in elevation can still fail if the open wall blocks the host stand, cuts a server lane, or parks panels in the only accessible path to the patio.

  • Folding: Plan which jamb receives the stack, whether the system splits, and whether a swing door is integrated for after-hours or single-person entry when the wall is closed.
  • Multi-slide: Plan track count, which panels are operable daily, whether panels pocket, and how much wall cavity or exterior stack depth the architect has actually drawn.
  • Both: Coordinate the structural header early. Operable glass walls are not a substitute for a designed load path above the opening.

Threshold and floor transition

Restaurants live or die on the sill. Guests cross it constantly. Staff roll bussing carts and highchairs across it. Rain and wash-down water hit it every week.

Folding and multi-slide systems both need a documented threshold strategy: flush vs raised, drainage path, interior floor finish termination, and exterior paving slope. Top-hung systems can reduce floor-track height but still need a weather and transition detail at the panel base. For flush-threshold accessibility, drainage, and floor coordination depth, use the companion draft flush glass-wall thresholds.

Stacking, parking, seating, and service access (absorbs B24)

Brief B24 (Glass-Wall Stacking Layouts: Protecting Seating, Circulation and Service Access) combines into this destination. The stacking section is not a separate thin URL.

Draw the wall in three states on the same plan: closed, partially open for lunch, and fully open for evening service. Then ask:

  1. Where do panels park when fully open?
  2. Does that park zone steal seats, block a fire exit path, or crowd the host stand?
  3. Can a server still pass with a tray when the stack is out?
  4. Is there a protected lane for dishware / bussing that does not cross the open weather line awkwardly?
  5. When the wall is closed, where is the operable leaf for staff and late guests?

Daily operations

A restaurant wall is opened and closed by staff, not by a marketing photo. Ask who opens it, how long it takes, and what happens in a sudden thunderstorm.

  • Staff count: Can one opener move the wall safely, or does the configuration need two people?
  • Locking sequence: Document the manufacturer sequence at handover. Do not leave it as tribal knowledge.
  • Weather decision: Who decides to close early, and how fast can panels return to weather seal?
  • HVAC / smoke: Coordinate dampers and any required smoke control so conditioned air and life-safety systems match the open-wall mode (see also the live folding Florida landing note on HVAC coordination).
  • Maintenance: Tracks collect sand and grit in coastal Florida. Build a cleaning habit into FOH / BOH SOPs.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the difference between folding and multi-slide glass walls for restaurants?

Folding (bi-fold) walls hinge panels together and stack accordion-style at one or both jambs. Multi-slide walls run panels on parallel tracks so they slide and stack or pocket. Both open indoor-outdoor dining. The choice is driven by stack location, threshold detail, daily staff operation, and service circulation.

Which system is better for restaurant service access?

Neither is universally better. Map the stack or pocket against host stand, server lanes, and egress before you choose. Folding often clears a wide continuous opening but parks a deep stack at the jamb. Multi-slide can keep a thinner stack or pocket panels, but needs track and pocket space planned early.

Does ACG install Euro-Wall systems in Florida?

Yes. Live ACG pages state that ACG is a Euro-Wall installer for commercial projects in Florida and installs Euro-Wall folding and multi-slide systems. Confirm the exact configuration and product approval for each project.

Where should stacking and panel parking be decided?

During design, on a plan that shows seating, circulation, and service paths with the wall open and closed. Do not leave stack location to field improvisation after furniture is laid out.
