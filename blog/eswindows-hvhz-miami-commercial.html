What Miami-Dade HVHZ GCs still need

On bid day, “ESWindows or equal” is not enough in the HVHZ. The permit path cares about the exact assembly: system, glass makeup, size, finish, and impact class against an active Miami-Dade Notice of Acceptance.

Florida GCs keep ESWindows on Miami-Dade and Broward commercial schedules when these conditions line up:

  • Commercial openings, not residential dealer work. Storefront, curtainwall, window wall, and commercial entrances on GC packages.
  • HVHZ approval geography. Miami-Dade / Broward path named on the drawings, not assumed from a non-HVHZ Florida Product Approval.
  • Impact class matched to elevation. Large Missile Impact on lower floors and Small Missile Impact higher up, per the elevation and the code path on the drawings.
  • One installer who also owns the NOA package. ACG furnishes and installs ESWindows commercial systems and packages the approval docs for the AHJ path named on the drawings.

If the schedule is specifically ES-8000 storefront outside the HVHZ decision problem, use the soft companion ESWindows ES-8000 Florida commercial installer post. This spoke stays on HVHZ commercial GC risk and approvals.

Approvals pathway without guessing numbers

Coastal Florida commercial work usually needs one of two product-approval paths. Inside Miami-Dade and Broward HVHZ, the controlling path is almost always:

  1. Miami-Dade / Broward HVHZ: active Miami-Dade Notice of Acceptance for the exact assembly used on the permit set.
  2. Other Florida jurisdictions: Florida Product Approval (or the AHJ-accepted equivalent) that covers the configuration. Do not paste a non-HVHZ listing into an HVHZ bid form.

ESWindows publishes impact and commercial product families with Florida approval documentation on active listings. ACG coordinates the product-approval package on installed scopes: the applicable listing, glazing specs, and installation drawings required by the permit path.

What we will not do in this article is paste a single NOA ID or design pressure and pretend it covers every ESWindows opening in Miami. Approvals are configuration-specific. GC bid risks on ESWindows HVHZ packages

Most HVHZ friction is paperwork and configuration control, not brand availability.

  • Brand without configuration. Naming ESWindows without locking the assembly that carries the active NOA creates a late substitution.
  • Wrong impact class by elevation. LMI vs SMI mistakes fail permit review even when the brand is correct.
  • Shops after glass is cut. Pre-glazed packages punish wrong rough openings and late hardware changes.
  • Split buyout and install. Material-only from one source and install from another leaves the GC bridging the NOA package and the field crew.
  • Copied numbers from a blog or old PDF. Listings expire and configurations change. Confirm on the current NOA / product sheet.

ACG’s value on an HVHZ ESWindows buy is not a slogan. It is one Florida licensed contractor running manufacturer coordination and field install so the GC is not bridging a distributor quote and a separate crew.

Installer coordination that protects the schedule

Commercial HVHZ install coordination is where bids turn into RFIs.

  • Shop drawings vs product data. Product sheets prove the system exists. Shops prove your openings. Release fabrication on approved shops, not on a brochure PDF.
  • Field measure before release. Who owns field verify must be explicit in the subcontract.
  • Door and hardware timing. Entrances, panic devices, closers, and access control must lock before glass is cut when doors are in the same package.
  • Finish and coastal exposure. Put the finish and warranty expectation in writing before color samples get lost in email.
  • Material-only vs furnish-and-install. ACG can quote both. Furnish-and-install puts buyout, submittals, receiving, install, and punch under one glazing sub. Say which mode you want on the same schedule.
