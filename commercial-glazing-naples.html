Coastal Performance

Wind, Debris, and Salt Air.
Naples-Grade Glazing.

Hurricane Ian demonstrated exactly what inadequate glazing looks like in the Southwest Florida coastal environment — and what happens to buildings whose glazing systems were not properly specified, installed, or documented. Collier County's post-Ian recovery and rebuilding program has raised the bar for what GCs expect from their glazing subs: systems that perform in the field as specified on paper, installations that hold to manufacturer standards, and documentation packages that stand up to post-storm scrutiny.

ACG specifies glazing systems to Florida's High-Velocity Hurricane Zone standards — large missile impact testing for coastal zones, wind load calculations matched to site-specific exposure categories, and Miami-Dade NOA documentation that covers every product in the assembly. For Naples projects within Wind Speed Zone regions requiring 160+ mph design pressure ratings, ACG specifies systems with the engineering to match — not systems that are approved for less demanding conditions and penciled in to pass a different spec.

Salt air is the silent failure mode in coastal glazing. Hardware that corrodes, finishes that pit, sealants that degrade — these failures start showing up within two to three years on buildings where the glazing sub specified to a price point rather than a performance requirement. ACG's Southwest Florida projects use marine-grade hardware, appropriate finish specifications for coastal exposure, and sealant selections that maintain bond in high-humidity, high-UV environments.

Common Questions

Naples Glazing FAQ.

Does ACG have a Naples office? +

Yes. ACG maintains a permanent Naples office serving Collier County and the Southwest Florida construction market. This is not an out-of-market sub making occasional trips south — ACG's Naples team is embedded in the local market, with local field crews, established relationships with Collier County building officials, and project managers who know the local GC network. When your superintendent needs something same-day, ACG's Naples team responds. We're not driving from West Palm Beach to solve a field issue.

What commercial glazing services does ACG provide in Naples? +

ACG installs impact windows and doors, commercial storefront systems, curtainwall, window wall, fire-rated glass assemblies, automatic entrances, interior glass partitions, and specialty glazing. All systems are HVHZ-certified and Florida Building Code compliant for Collier County coastal exposure requirements. ACG manages the full scope from design-assist through field installation — shop drawings, submittals, product approvals, fabrication coordination, and punch list completion.

What areas of Collier County does ACG serve? +

ACG serves all of Collier County — Naples (Fifth Avenue South, Mercato, Pelican Bay, Park Shore, Old Naples, North Naples), Marco Island, Ave Maria, Bonita Springs, Estero, the Immokalee Road corridor, and the Collier Boulevard commercial zone. ACG's Southwest Florida coverage also includes Lee County, Fort Myers, and Cape Coral. If it's in Southwest Florida, ACG's Naples office covers it.

How fast does ACG turn around a scope for Naples projects? +

Send us your drawings and ACG returns a detailed scope — system recommendations, quantities, and Division 08 scope breakdown — within 48 hours. ACG's estimating process eliminates the manual bottlenecks that cause delays at other glazing subs. No vague allowances, no hidden exclusions, no three-week wait for a number that turns out to be incomplete. Send plans Monday, have a complete scope Wednesday. We price to win work in Collier County, not to pad a margin on a sub we expect to negotiate down.

Is Naples in the HVHZ zone, and is ACG certified for it? +

Portions of Collier County — particularly coastal Naples, Marco Island, and beachside zones — fall under High-Velocity Hurricane Zone (HVHZ) designation. HVHZ projects require glazing systems with Miami-Dade Notice of Acceptance (NOA) documentation and Florida product approvals. ACG is fully certified for HVHZ work and manages the complete documentation stack — Miami-Dade NOA packages, Florida product approvals, shop drawings, and submittal packages built to pass Collier County plan review the first time. Non-HVHZ Collier County projects still require impact-rated systems per Florida Building Code; ACG handles both designation types routinely.
