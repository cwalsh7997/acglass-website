Florida Product Approval explained — FPA vs NOA, when each applies

Most Florida commercial glazing projects outside Miami-Dade and Broward run on the Florida Product Approval system, not the NOA system. They're similar in purpose but different in authority, test standards, and database. This page explains what FPA is, how to look up products, how it compares to NOA, and the errors that most commonly appear on non-HVHZ specs.

By Connor Walsh · President, American Commercial Glass · FAA Pilot · Published May 13, 2026

The short answer

Florida Product Approval (FPA) is the statewide approval system for hurricane-resistant fenestration in Florida, issued by the Florida Building Commission at floridabuilding.org. It is required outside HVHZ. Inside HVHZ (Miami-Dade and Broward), only Miami-Dade NOAs apply. FPA uses ASTM/AAMA test standards; NOA uses Miami-Dade TAS standards. A product can hold both, but the approvals are not interchangeable between zones.

What Florida Product Approval is

The issuing authority

Florida Product Approval is administered by the Florida Building Commission, a division of the Florida Department of Business and Professional Regulation. The Commission maintains the Florida Product Approval database at floridabuilding.org, where manufacturers submit test reports, and the Commission reviews and approves products for statewide use under the Florida Building Code.

FPA is a statewide system. An approval issued through floridabuilding.org is valid for permit submissions in every Florida county except where a more restrictive local requirement applies — specifically HVHZ, where Miami-Dade NOAs supersede FPA for exterior fenestration. No other Florida county has a more restrictive county-level product approval system comparable to Miami-Dade's NOA process, though some coastal municipalities may have adopted supplemental requirements through local amendments.

What FPA covers

FPA covers a wide range of building products — not just glazing. For commercial glazing, the relevant product categories are: exterior windows and doors (impact-resistant), exterior storefront and curtainwall systems, skylights and roof systems, and structural glazing systems. Each approved product receives a Florida Product Number (FL number), which appears in the floridabuilding.org database and must be referenced in permit submissions.

Like the Miami-Dade NOA system, FPA approves specific configurations: a specific frame series, glass type, glass thickness, and maximum unit dimensions, tested at specific design pressures. Substituting components requires the same engineer-of-record review process as an NOA deviation.

FPA and the Florida Building Code wind-borne debris region

The Florida Building Code designates wind-borne debris regions based on design wind speeds from the Florida wind speed maps. Most of coastal and central Florida falls within wind-borne debris regions where impact protection or approved shutters are required for exterior openings. The specific requirements vary by jurisdiction, building occupancy type, and opening location. FPA is the mechanism by which products demonstrate they meet those requirements statewide. Confirm the applicable wind speed and exposure category with the project EOR — the Florida Building Code provides the current wind maps, and the 8th Edition is the base code as of 2026.

How to look up FPAs at floridabuilding.org

The product approval search

The Florida Product Approval search is at floridabuilding.org/pr/pr_app_lst.aspx. Search fields include: manufacturer name, product category (e.g., "Exterior Doors, Windows"), FL number, and product name. Results show the FL number, product description, current approval status, and the date of approval. Select a product to see the full approval record, including test reports, approved configurations, and the approval certificate.

What to verify in the FPA record

For each product, verify: (1) the approval is in an active status; (2) the specific frame series and configuration you are specifying are listed as approved; (3) the design pressure rating meets or exceeds the project's required DP; (4) the maximum unit dimensions in the approval cover the unit sizes on the drawings. The FPA record also lists the testing laboratory, the specific test standards used, and the test report dates.

FPA approvals do not expire in the same way Miami-Dade NOAs do. However, approvals can be revoked or superseded if a product is modified or if the Florida Building Code changes the applicable test requirements. Always pull a current record from floridabuilding.org at the time of permit submission.

Testing protocols — what FPA requires

FPA uses established ASTM and AAMA test standards, which are the same standards used nationally for impact-resistant fenestration. This is a significant difference from the Miami-Dade NOA system, which uses Miami-Dade's proprietary TAS standards. The test standards for FPA-approved impact fenestration include:

Test standardWhat it coversNotes
ASTM E1886Performance of exterior windows, curtain walls, doors, and storm shutters under large and small missile impact and cyclic pressure loadingThe primary impact test standard for FPA; functionally similar to TAS 201/203 but not identical
ASTM E1996Specification for performance of exterior windows, curtain walls, doors, and storm shutters impacted by windborne debris in hurricanesDefines the missile test levels and acceptable performance criteria
ASTM E330Structural performance under uniform static air pressure differenceMeasures deflection and structural capacity under design loads
ASTM E331Water penetration of exterior windows, skylights, doors, and curtain wallsWater infiltration under pressure differential

The testing differences between FPA and NOA matter because TAS 201/203 cycle count and missile specifications are different from ASTM E1886/E1996. A product that passes ASTM E1886 testing for FPA approval has not necessarily been tested to the TAS 201/203 standard that HVHZ requires.

When FPA is required on a Florida commercial project

The wind-borne debris region rule

The Florida Building Code requires impact protection for exterior openings in wind-borne debris regions. The wind-borne debris region is defined by the design wind speed maps in the Florida Building Code — the 8th Edition maps designate most of coastal Florida within 1 mile of the coast in wind speed zones above 130 mph as requiring impact protection. Inland areas may have different requirements. Check the current Florida Building Code wind speed map for the specific project address and confirm with the local AHJ.

Projects where FPA is most commonly required

On Florida commercial projects in Palm Beach, Sarasota, Lee, Collier, Charlotte, Martin, St. Lucie, Brevard, Volusia, and similar coastal counties, FPA-listed impact assemblies are the standard requirement. Inland projects in central Florida — Orange, Polk, Hillsborough, Pinellas — may have different requirements depending on the exact wind speed and exposure category. On projects in our West Palm Beach, Naples, and Tampa markets, we verify FPA status on every product we specify.

Non-impact projects with FPA structural ratings

Not every Florida project requires impact glazing. Projects with FBC-compliant shutters or protection systems for each opening may use non-impact fenestration. However, the fenestration still needs to meet structural performance requirements (design pressure, deflection limits). Even non-impact products must have an FPA or equivalent structural certification demonstrating they meet the project's wind load requirements.

How to verify an installer's FPA-listed products

What to ask for and where to verify it

When reviewing a glazing contractor's submittal for a non-HVHZ Florida project, request the FL numbers for every product being installed. Cross-reference each FL number at floridabuilding.org to confirm: the product is active, the configuration matches what is being installed, and the rated DP meets or exceeds the project's required DP. For curtainwall systems, verify the maximum panel dimensions match the installed units.

FPA on submittals

A properly prepared submittal for a non-HVHZ Florida commercial project should include the FL number for each product, the product cut sheet confirming the specific configuration, the test report reference for the rated DP, and the engineer-of-record's confirmation that the product meets the project's required DP. If any of these elements are missing, the submittal is incomplete for permit purposes. We structure every non-HVHZ Florida submittal to include all four elements — it eliminates re-submittal cycles that add weeks to the schedule.

Common errors on non-HVHZ Florida projects

Specifying products with HVHZ NOAs but no FPA on non-HVHZ projects

Some NOA-listed products also hold Florida Product Approvals — but not all do. A product that is NOA-listed but does not have a Florida Product Approval is technically not listed for the FPA database, which some AHJs outside HVHZ require. In practice, most AHJs outside HVHZ will accept an NOA in lieu of an FPA because an NOA implies a more stringent testing standard. Confirm with the local AHJ before submitting, and include the NOA documentation even if the AHJ accepts it in lieu of FPA.

Using out-of-state products without FPA documentation

Manufacturers based outside Florida sometimes sell products in Florida without a Florida Product Approval. The product may comply with ASTM E1886 and E1996 and carry impact ratings, but without an FL number in the floridabuilding.org database, it is not approved for Florida use under the Florida Building Code. This comes up on projects in Central Florida and North Florida where contractors source products from regional suppliers unfamiliar with Florida's specific approval requirements.

Treating FPA as equivalent to NOA on projects near the HVHZ boundary

Projects near the Broward/Palm Beach county line occasionally have confusion about which approval system applies. The boundary is the county line. Any project address in Broward County requires NOA, not FPA. Any project address in Palm Beach County requires FPA unless a local amendment imposes NOA requirements — which you must confirm with the specific municipality's building department. Do not assume; verify.

Not checking for FPA approval on specific configurations

The same configuration-specificity rule applies to FPA as to NOA. The FPA covers specific tested assemblies. If you are installing a unit larger than the FPA's tested maximum dimension, or using a glass thickness not listed in the FPA's approved glass list, the FPA does not cover that installation. This applies even if the product brand and product name match the FPA record.

Frequently asked questions

What is Florida Product Approval?

Florida Product Approval (FPA) is a statewide approval system administered by the Florida Building Commission for hurricane-resistant building products. FPA approvals are listed at floridabuilding.org and are required for impact-resistant fenestration on commercial and residential projects in Florida's wind-borne debris regions outside HVHZ.

Is Florida Product Approval the same as a Miami-Dade NOA?

No. FPA is issued by the Florida Building Commission using ASTM/AAMA test standards. Miami-Dade NOA is issued by Miami-Dade County using TAS test standards. NOA is required in HVHZ; FPA is required outside HVHZ. A product with only an FPA cannot be used in HVHZ exterior openings.

Where do I look up Florida Product Approvals?

At floridabuilding.org/pr/pr_app_lst.aspx. Search by manufacturer, product category, FL number, or product name. Always pull the current record directly from the Florida Building Commission database — do not rely on manufacturer-provided documentation.

What testing does Florida Product Approval require?

FPA impact products are tested to ASTM E1886 (large and small missile impact and cyclic loading) and ASTM E1996, plus ASTM E330 (structural performance), ASTM E331 (water infiltration), and ASTM E283 (air infiltration). Testing is conducted at accredited laboratories and witnessed per Florida Building Commission protocol.

Do I need Florida Product Approval outside HVHZ?

Yes, in most cases. The Florida Building Code requires FPA-listed impact fenestration in wind-borne debris regions, which covers most of coastal and central Florida. The specific requirement depends on the project's location, wind speed, and exposure category. Confirm with the local AHJ and the project EOR. See the current Florida Building Code wind speed maps for the definitive coverage area.
