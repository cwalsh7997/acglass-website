Miami-Dade NOA explained — how to read, verify, and apply

The NOA is the single most important document in HVHZ commercial glazing. If you can't read one, you can't spec correctly, bid correctly, or submit for permit correctly. This page covers what an NOA is, how to find it, what its cover sheet tells you, and where GCs and architects make the most common errors.

By Connor Walsh · President, American Commercial Glass · FL CGC #1531993 · FAA Pilot · Published May 13, 2026

The short answer

A Miami-Dade NOA (Notice of Acceptance) is the official approval issued by Miami-Dade Product Control certifying that a tested assembly is approved for HVHZ installation. Search it at miamidade.gov/building. The NOA number, expiration date, rated DP, and approved configurations are all on the cover sheet. The assembly must be installed exactly as specified — no component substitutions without EOR sign-off.

What an NOA technically is

The issuing authority

An NOA — Notice of Acceptance — is issued by the Miami-Dade County Building and Neighborhood Services Department, Product Control Division. It is not a statewide document and it is not issued by the Florida Building Commission. The Miami-Dade Product Control Division is a separate authority that has maintained its own testing and approval system since the post-Andrew building code reforms of the mid-1990s. The Florida Building Code recognizes Miami-Dade NOAs as the required approval for HVHZ fenestration installations in Miami-Dade and Broward counties.

What it certifies

An NOA certifies a specific assembly — not a product line and not a component. The tested assembly includes: the specific frame series and extrusion profile, the glass type and nominal thickness, the glass interlayer (for laminated assemblies), the spacer type, the anchorage pattern and fastener size, and the installation method. All of these variables are fixed at the time of testing. Changing any one of them produces a new, untested assembly that the NOA does not cover.

This is the most important thing to understand about NOAs. It is not enough for a product to have an NOA. The specific configuration you are installing must be listed in the NOA's approved configurations. We verify this on every HVHZ submittal — and we find discrepancies on roughly one in four projects where the design team has spec'd products without confirming specific configurations.

The difference from Florida Product Approval

Florida Product Approval (FPA) is the statewide system for non-HVHZ projects. FPAs are issued by the Florida Building Commission via floridabuilding.org. They use ASTM and AAMA test standards rather than Miami-Dade's TAS standards. A product with an FPA but no Miami-Dade NOA cannot be used in HVHZ exterior openings. See our dedicated page on Florida Product Approval explained for the full FPA vs. NOA breakdown.

How to find a Miami-Dade NOA

The official database

The Miami-Dade Product Control database is at miamidade.gov/building/pc-search.asp. You can search by manufacturer, product name, product category, or NOA number. The search returns a list of matching NOAs with their current status — active or expired. Select an NOA to download the full PDF.

Always download directly from the Miami-Dade site. Manufacturer websites often host PDF copies of their NOAs, but the authoritative version for permit submission is the one at miamidade.gov. Building departments in Miami-Dade and Broward will reject submissions that reference expired NOAs or NOA documents pulled from manufacturer sites if there is a version discrepancy.

If a product's NOA is expired

An expired NOA cannot be used for new permit submissions. If the product you are specifying shows an expired NOA, contact the manufacturer directly to determine whether a renewal is in process. Some manufacturers let NOAs lapse on discontinued product lines and maintain current NOAs only on their active products. If the product is no longer NOA-active, you need to select an alternative.

How to read an NOA cover sheet

The cover sheet of every NOA contains the same standard information in a consistent format. Here is what each field means and why it matters for specification:

Subsequent pages of the NOA

The cover sheet is followed by multiple sections. Installation drawings show the approved anchorage patterns, jamb/head/sill details, and approved frame orientations. The glass list table shows every approved glass type, thickness, and interlayer. The anchor table shows approved fastener sizes, embedment depths, and maximum spacing. All of these are part of the approved assembly. Deviating from any of them — even by a fraction of an inch on anchor spacing — means the installation is no longer covered by the NOA.

Approved configurations vs. field substitutions

Why "same manufacturer" is not enough

One of the most common errors in HVHZ commercial glazing is treating an NOA as a blanket approval for a manufacturer's product line. An NOA covers specific tested configurations — and a single manufacturer may have a dozen different NOAs covering different product lines, frame sizes, and glass combinations. ESWindows, for example, maintains separate NOAs for the ES7000 series and the ES8000 series, with different approved configurations within each NOA.

Field substitutions — when they happen and what to do

A field substitution occurs when the installed assembly deviates from the NOA-approved specification. Common causes: the specified glass thickness is out of stock and a substitute is used; the frame series is updated mid-project and the new series has a different NOA; the anchor spacing specified in the NOA doesn't coordinate with the substrate. Any substitution requires the engineer of record to review and certify the equivalency in writing before the AHJ will accept the deviation. This is not a field decision — it is a design decision that requires licensed engineering review.

NOA expiration and renewal cycles

Miami-Dade NOAs have a five-year term. The expiration date is printed on the cover sheet. Manufacturers must submit renewal applications to the Miami-Dade Product Control Division before expiration. A renewal may require additional testing if the product has been modified, or it may be renewed on the basis of unchanged test data with updated manufacturer certification.

Projects with long design timelines — 18 to 24 months from schematic design to permit submission — should verify NOA status at permit submission, not at the time of specification. An NOA that was current in design development may expire before permit review is complete.

How to spec an NOA-listed assembly correctly

The correct spec sequence

Start with the project DP requirements from the structural engineer. Identify products with NOAs rated at or above that DP. Verify that the NOA covers the specific frame size, glass thickness, and maximum unit dimensions your project requires. Reference the NOA number in the spec section, not just the product name. Include the approved configuration table from the NOA as an exhibit in the submittal package.

Writing the spec section

In Division 08 (Openings), call out the specific NOA number alongside the product reference. Example language: "Aluminum storefront: ESWindows ES7000, Miami-Dade NOA [reference current NOA number from miamidade.gov at time of submission], rated for minimum [project DP] PSF positive and [project DP] PSF negative per NOA approved configuration. Glass shall be laminated per NOA glass list, [thickness] nominal, [interlayer type]." This level of specificity in the spec prevents substitution errors during procurement and installation.

Common errors GCs make reading NOAs

Assuming the NOA number on the product label is the current NOA

Manufacturers print NOA numbers on product labels, but those labels may reference previous NOA versions. The product may have been tested under a legacy NOA that has since been superseded or revised. Always pull the current NOA from miamidade.gov using the manufacturer and product name — do not rely on label printing.

Not verifying maximum unit size

NOAs specify maximum unit dimensions — the largest opening width and height that the tested assembly was approved for. A project with large-format storefront units or wide curtainwall bays may exceed the maximum unit size in the NOA, even if the frame series and glass spec are correct. Exceeding the NOA's maximum unit size means the assembly is not approved. This requires either a different product selection or an EOR analysis demonstrating that the larger unit meets the structural requirements through engineering calculation rather than tested NOA coverage.

Reading the cover sheet DP as a project DP recommendation

The NOA's rated DP is the maximum the tested assembly achieved — not a recommendation for what your project needs. Your project DP is determined by the structural engineer per ASCE 7. If your project DP is +100/–115 PSF, you need an NOA rated at or above those values in both directions. A product NOA rated at +85/–95 PSF does not cover a +100/–115 PSF project, regardless of the manufacturer's claims.

Specifying based on a manufacturer's product sheet rather than the NOA

Manufacturer product sheets market the capabilities of a product line. They are not the controlling document for permit submission. The NOA is. Product sheets may reference a highest-rated DP across all configurations; your specific configuration may be rated lower. Read the NOA, not the sell sheet.

Real-world examples: ESWindows and Euro-Wall

ESWindows ES7000 — NOA 23-0724.09

The ESWindows ES7000 series holds Miami-Dade NOA 23-0724.09 (see miamidade.gov/building for current status and any revisions). The ES7000 is a commercial storefront and entrance system. The NOA covers specific configurations of the ES7000 frame with approved glass types, thicknesses, and interlayers. We install ES7000 under this NOA on HVHZ storefront work throughout Miami-Dade and Broward. Verify the current NOA status and expiration date at miamidade.gov before specifying — NOA numbers are verified at the time of permit submission.

Euro-Wall Vista series

Euro-Wall maintains Miami-Dade NOAs for its Vista Multi Slide, Vista Fold, Vista Pivot, and DirectSet series. Each product line has its own NOA covering its approved configurations, design pressure ratings, and maximum unit sizes. The Euro-Wall Vista NOAs cover large-format openings that are common in high-end hospitality and residential commercial work in HVHZ. Frequently asked questions

What is a Miami-Dade NOA?

A Miami-Dade Notice of Acceptance (NOA) is the official approval document issued by the Miami-Dade County Product Control Division certifying that a specific assembly has passed TAS testing and is approved for installation in HVHZ. Required for every exterior fenestration assembly in Miami-Dade and Broward counties. Search at miamidade.gov/building.

How do I look up a Miami-Dade NOA?

Search the Miami-Dade Product Control database at miamidade.gov/building/pc-search.asp. Search by manufacturer name, product name, or NOA number. Download the current PDF directly from the Miami-Dade site for any permit submission. Do not rely on manufacturer-hosted copies.

When does a Miami-Dade NOA expire?

NOAs have a five-year term. The expiration date appears on the cover sheet. Always verify expiration at the time of permit submission — not at the time of specification — particularly on projects with 12+ month design timelines.

Can I substitute glass on an NOA-listed assembly?

No, not without a signed/sealed equivalency letter from the engineer of record. The NOA's approved glass list is fixed at the time of testing. Substituting glass type, thickness, or interlayer without EOR certification voids NOA coverage for that installation and will cause the inspection to fail.

What is the difference between an NOA and a Florida Product Approval?

An NOA is issued by Miami-Dade County and uses Miami-Dade TAS test standards. It is required in HVHZ (Miami-Dade and Broward). A Florida Product Approval (FPA) is issued by the Florida Building Commission and uses ASTM/AAMA test standards. FPA is the statewide non-HVHZ approval. A product can hold both; the NOA controls in HVHZ.

What information is on an NOA cover sheet?

The cover sheet shows the NOA number, expiration date, manufacturer, product description, approved use, design pressure rating, and approved configuration summary. Subsequent pages contain installation drawings, approved glass lists, and anchorage tables. Every page of the NOA is part of the required assembly specification.
