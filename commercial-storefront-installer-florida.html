What a commercial storefront is

Commercial storefront is the aluminum or thermally-broken framing system at the ground-floor exterior of a commercial building — typically supporting 1-inch insulating glass units in extrusions 2 to 4 inches deep. It is the standard specification for retail tenants, restaurants, hotel and multifamily lobbies, vestibules, medical office entries, public sector main entrances, and Class A office ground-floor envelopes. Storefront is distinct from curtainwall (which hangs in front of the structural slabs on mid-rise and high-rise buildings) and window wall (which spans between slabs on multifamily and mid-rise applications).

A complete commercial storefront installation includes the framing extrusions, the glass units, the perimeter sealant and gasket system, anchorage to the structural substrate, integrated entrance doors (with hardware), optional automatic-operator integration, and — in Florida — a current product approval documenting compliance with the wind-load and impact requirements of the Florida Building Code: a Miami-Dade NOA inside HVHZ, or a statewide Florida Product Approval everywhere else.

What ACG includes in a storefront scope. Frame, glass make-up, hardware, automatic operator integration where specified, sealant, anchorage, water testing, and full submittal package with NOA / FPA documentation. ACG can self-perform or coordinate with the GC's hollow-metal door subcontractor, hardware spec writer, and Allegion hardware integrator. Florida code & permitting for commercial storefront

Every commercial storefront installed in Florida must meet the relevant chapters of the Florida Building Code (FBC). The most relevant sections for storefront:

  • FBC Section 1609 — wind loads and protection of openings; references ASCE 7 for wind load calculation.
  • FBC Section 1609.1.2 — protection of exterior glazed openings in Wind-Borne Debris Region (WBDR) and High Velocity Hurricane Zone (HVHZ).
  • FBC Section 1709 — structural testing; references TAS 201, 202, 203 for impact-resistant glazing.
  • FBC Chapter 24 — glass and glazing requirements; safety glazing locations.
  • ANSI Z97.1 / CPSC 16 CFR 1201 — safety glazing standards.

Storefront systems installed in Miami-Dade or Broward (HVHZ) must carry current Miami-Dade NOAs. Storefront systems installed in WBDR coastal jurisdictions outside HVHZ must carry Florida Product Approval (FPA). Both categories require submittal documentation including the manufacturer system, the glass make-up, the anchorage detail, the design pressure analysis, and the active approval document. Full Florida HVHZ reference: Florida HVHZ Glazing Requirements. Full NOA reference: Florida NOA Explained.

Which approval path applies to your jurisdiction

The single most common source of storefront submittal rejection in Florida is submitting the wrong approval type for the county. HVHZ is not statewide — it is defined in the Florida Building Code as Miami-Dade and Broward counties only. Everywhere else in Florida runs on statewide Florida Product Approval.

JurisdictionApproval document requiredTest protocol
HVHZ — Miami-Dade, BrowardMiami-Dade County Notice of Acceptance (NOA)TAS 201 / 202 / 203
WBDR, non-HVHZ — coastal counties incl. Palm Beach, Lee, Collier, Duval, PinellasFlorida Product Approval (statewide) with impact ratingASTM E1886 / E1996
Inland, non-WBDRFlorida Product Approval (statewide), design pressure onlyASTM E330 structural, E283 air, E331 water

ACG confirms the applicable path at bid time, not at submittal time, so the system named in the proposal is the system the AHJ will accept. Related: Florida Product Approval vs NOA · Storefront FBC 1609 compliance · TAS 201/202/203 testing.

Procurement & submittal coordination for general contractors

On most Florida commercial projects the storefront package is not the largest line item, but it is one of the most schedule-sensitive: it sits on the critical path to dry-in, it carries long fabrication lead times, and it is the package most likely to be rejected on first submittal. What follows is how ACG runs the paperwork side of a Division 08 scope.

What ACG needs in the bid package

  • Architectural elevations and door/window schedules, plus the glazing details.
  • Structural drawings sufficient to confirm the substrate and anchorage condition at each opening type.
  • Division 08 specification sections — 08 41 13 (aluminum-framed entrances and storefronts), 08 44 13 (glazed aluminum curtain wall) where the scope crosses over, and 08 80 00 (glazing).
  • Project wind-load criteria: risk category, ultimate design wind speed, exposure category, and the design pressures scheduled per opening.
  • Schedule milestones — specifically the dry-in date and the date the GC needs approved shop drawings back.

What the submittal package contains

  • Shop drawings — plans, elevations, and full-scale details at head, jamb, sill, and intermediate horizontals; anchorage type and spacing keyed to the structural condition.
  • Product approval — the active NOA or Florida Product Approval number, with the approval's own design-pressure table shown against the scheduled pressures so the reviewer can compare them without leaving the sheet.
  • Glass make-up — lite thicknesses, interlayer type and thickness, coating, spacer, and the performance values (U-factor, SHGC, VLT) matched to the energy compliance path.
  • Hardware schedule — keyed to the specifier's hardware sets, including automatic operator power and controls coordination where an Allegion package is specified.
  • Structural calculations signed and sealed by a Florida-registered engineer where the AHJ or the specification requires site-specific anchorage calculations.
  • Finish samples, sealant compatibility and adhesion data, and mockup scope where the spec calls for one.

Where storefront submittals usually get rejected

  1. Approval type mismatch — an NOA submitted into a non-HVHZ county, or a statewide approval submitted into Miami-Dade or Broward.
  2. Expired approval — NOAs and Florida Product Approvals both carry expiration dates, and a renewal in progress is not an active approval. ACG verifies currency at bid and re-verifies before submittal.
  3. Design pressure shortfall at corner zones — the field-zone pressure clears but the corner-zone pressure does not, because the zone map was not applied opening by opening.
  4. Anchorage outside the tested condition — the approval was tested into a substrate the project does not have, which is what drives the need for site-specific sealed calculations.
  5. Missing safety-glazing identification at hazardous locations under FBC Chapter 24.

Bid → submittal → install process

  1. Bid invitation received — GC sends drawings (architectural + structural), specifications (Division 08), schedule, NOA list (if specified), and contact info.
  2. ACG returns bid in 5 business days — line-item proposal with named manufacturer, system family, glass make-up, hardware, NOA / FPA reference, schedule milestones, value-engineering alternates.
  3. Award + contract — AIA-format subcontract, COI exchanged.
  4. Submittal package within 2–3 weeks — shop drawings, glass make-up, NOA / FPA documentation, anchorage details, hardware schedule, sealant schedule, finish samples, mockup if required.
  5. Manufacturer lead time 8–14 weeks — depending on manufacturer, system, glass make-up, and finish.
  6. Field mobilization 1 week ahead — substrate verification, rough opening tolerance check per ASTM E2112, crew briefing.
  7. Installation — daily pre-task plans, weekly tool-box talks, GC-coordinated schedule integration.
  8. Water testing — perimeter and glazing per ASTM E1105 when required by spec or owner.
  9. Punch and closeout — punch-list completion, COI, NOA / FPA documentation, warranty package, signed water-test reports, O&M handover.

RFI response SLA: 48 hours standard; same-day for field-urgent. Bid response: 5 business days for complete packages.

What's the difference between storefront and curtainwall?

Storefront is ground-floor, supported by building structure, 2–4 inch framing, used for retail / restaurants / lobbies. Curtainwall hangs in front of structural slabs on mid-rise and high-rise, with 6–12 inch framing, used for office and hotel exteriors.
