The Market

Cape Coral is a commercial glazing market.

Cape Coral is the most extensive city in Southwest Florida by area - 120 square miles, over 400 miles of navigable canals, and a population exceeding 215,000. The commercial market spans downtown Cape Coral, Cape Harbour waterfront retail, Tarpon Point Marina, Pine Island Road corridor retail, Del Prado Boulevard medical and professional, and substantial Class A multifamily along the Caloosahatchee River and Bimini Basin waterfronts.

Hurricane Ian (September 2022) brought catastrophic damage throughout Cape Coral. Storm surge swept through the southern portions of the city, and wind damage was widespread. Hurricane Milton (October 2024) added further commercial impact. Cape Coral's commercial rebuild and hardening cycle requires Florida Building Code 8th Edition compliance, documented Florida Product Approval, and DP ratings appropriate to Exposure C (most of Cape Coral) or Exposure D (waterfront elevations).

ACG serves Cape Coral from our Naples office (45 miles south) with the ESWindows flagship partnership. ESWindows 8000 storefront, 9000 window wall, and SGD-2020 impact sliding doors carry Florida Product Approval for Exposure C and D conditions. For waterfront hospitality, restaurant, and amenity applications where operable walls open toward the canals, we install Euro-Wall folding and multi-slide systems. Our referenced SW Florida portfolio includes Illumina Fort Myers luxury multifamily and Collier County master-planned community amenity buildings.

Cape Coral FAQ

Common questions - answered.

Does Cape Coral require impact windows on commercial buildings?

Yes. Cape Coral and Lee County sit in Florida's wind-borne debris region. Florida Building Code 8th Edition requires impact-rated glazing or approved shutter systems on every commercial opening. Post-Hurricane Ian, the City of Cape Coral and Lee County have been especially rigorous on rebuild permits, with most projects requiring full impact glazing.

What exposure category applies to Cape Coral commercial projects?

Most of Cape Coral inland is Exposure Category C. Waterfront elevations along the Caloosahatchee River, Bimini Basin, Cape Harbour, Tarpon Point, and canal-facing sites are Exposure Category D with higher design pressures. DP40-DP60 is typical inland; DP60-DP80+ waterfront. Florida Product Approval documentation must confirm the appropriate exposure.

How did Hurricane Ian affect Cape Coral commercial buildings?

Hurricane Ian (September 2022) brought devastating wind and storm surge to Cape Coral, with the southern portions of the city experiencing significant storm surge inundation. Commercial damage was widespread across retail, hospitality, multifamily, and medical buildings. The rebuild and hardening cycle since has driven substantial demand for impact glazing upgrades and full FBC 8th Edition compliance.

Can ACG handle Cape Coral rebuild and new construction projects?

Yes. ACG serves Cape Coral from our Naples office with the same commercial glazing capability we bring to Collier County. Our ESWindows partnership provides the Exposure D-capable product depth Cape Coral waterfront projects require, and our documented operating system means insurance-carrier packages, submittals, and closeout documentation are delivered cleanly. Our referenced SW Florida portfolio includes Illumina Fort Myers luxury multifamily.
