How do you evaluate a Florida glazing subcontractor?

On five things: an active DBPR license, a current NOA or Florida Product Approval for the exact assembly on your elevation, bond capacity confirmed in writing by the surety, an additional insured endorsement rather than just a certificate, and whether the crew is direct hire or subcontracted. Every one is checkable from a public source, including the row about ACG.

  1. The license. Florida DBPR keeps a public licensee search. Type the number. You get status, expiry, the qualifying individual and any discipline. A number that does not resolve is worth asking about directly.
  2. The product approval. Every impact assembly needs either a Miami-Dade NOA or a Florida Product Approval, and both are public. Ask which approval number covers the exact configuration on your elevation, then read it. Approvals expire, and an expired one on a submittal is a plans examiner rejection waiting to happen.
  3. The bond. Ask for the single and aggregate capacity and the surety's name, in writing from the surety or the agent, not from the subcontractor. This one takes days, not minutes.
  4. The insurance. A certificate naming your entity as additional insured, with the endorsement attached. The certificate alone is not the endorsement.
  5. The crew. Ask who installs: direct employees, or subcontracted labor. Both can work. Only one of them is who you think you hired.

The license and the product approval take about ten minutes between them. The bond and the insurance depend on somebody else's turnaround. For the long version of each criterion, see how to compare Florida commercial glaziers.

What the table does not tell you.

A table of published facts is a filter. Four things it cannot show, including one that counts against ACG.

ACG is the newest firm here

Founded February 2021, so about five and a half years as of this page. Every other firm in the table has been trading longer, Southern Glass Products since 1968 and JEM by its own account more than forty years. If your evaluation weights time in business above everything else, that is a real mark against ACG, and it should be said plainly rather than left for a table to hide.

Counties served is a claim, not a capability

Any firm can list counties. What matters is whether they have installed there, whether they hold the product approvals that county's plans examiner expects, and whether their crew travels. Ask for a project in your county, by name.

A residential mix is not a fault

Ashe Glass & Mirror leads with shower enclosures, table tops and mirrored walls alongside its commercial work, and Southern Glass Products serves residential as well. That is a business model, not a defect. It matters only if your package has to be sequenced against a construction schedule and a submittal log, which is project administration rather than installation skill.

None of this predicts responsiveness

How fast a bid, an RFI or a shop drawing revision comes back is the thing that decides whether a subcontractor is easy to build with, and no published fact predicts it. The only test is to send a real scope and time the reply.
